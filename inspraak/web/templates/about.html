About and method

Inspraak (Dutch for having a say) is a consultation desk for Brussels organisations. It joins three public sources on one key, the EU Transparency Register ID, and puts a small team of agents on top. This page says who built it, how it works, what it does not do, and where the numbers come from.

Who built it

I'm Kaan Goker. I did the Advanced Master of Artificial Intelligence at KU Leuven and specialised in natural language processing and speech recognition. EU consultations looked like a good test for that: the data is public, but it sits on three sites and in many languages, and a small organisation rarely has a free day to read it all. I wanted to see how much of that reading a few agents with strict sourcing rules could do, and where they still get it wrong.

Source code on GitHub (MIT licence). kaangoker.dev. LinkedIn. Corrections: nadirkaangoker@gmail.com.

How it works

A supervisor routes one run per organisation through seven agents. It decides on what the run already holds and calls no model. The run stops twice for a person: at go / no-go, before anything is drafted, and again at draft review. Its state lives in a Postgres checkpointer, so a run can wait days for a decision.

  1. Profiler. The organisation's own register entry (goals, interests, category, budget), shown as registered, plus its earlier submissions in the snapshot. A reviewer can correct the profile; corrections are stored as memory, the register is never changed.
  2. Matcher. Open Have Your Say consultations are matched to the profile by embedding similarity (a small multilingual model, run locally), with a penalty for consultations that sit close to almost every profile. Consultations of the same initiative share one card. The 15 strongest are then judged by a language model in one call per organisation: a relevance between 0 and 1 and a one-line reason, shown on the card and cached, so reading a page never calls a model. "Not our topic" decisions exclude a consultation and lower the score of similar ones next time.
  3. Landscape. Who already submitted, counted by the stakeholder type and country each submitter declared on the portal. For organisation submissions one model call extracts a stance and up to three key asks with a verbatim quote; a quote that cannot be found character for character in the submission is dropped. Stance labels under 0.6 confidence are held back for review.
  4. Lobby mapper. Meetings from the Commission's file whose recorded subject is close to the consultation's title and summary. The subject is short free text, so this is marked approximate everywhere it appears. Counts by register category of the interest representative, never a label such as "ally" or "opponent".
  5. Reader. The Commission document, page by page. Passages closest to the profile are cited to the page of the PDF.
  6. Drafter. Written only after a person says go, within the 4,000 character limit of the portal, in the organisation's own voice (its earlier submissions).
  7. Critic. Checks every sentence of the draft and removes what it cannot source. A fact needs a Commission passage. A position (we support, we call for, the Commission must) needs the organisation's own past submission or register goals; a Commission passage alone does not make it the organisation's view. The draft then waits for a person. Nothing is ever submitted to the Commission by this system.

Limits, stated plainly

  • Everything runs from a local snapshot{% if snapshot_date %} (taken {{ snapshot_date | datetime }}, Brussels time){% endif %}. A consultation published later is missing, and counts and deadlines can have moved since. Deadlines are shown as dates; the countdown is worked out in your browser.
  • The Have Your Say endpoints are the portal's own backend, not a documented API.
  • Scope: consultations closing within 90 days plus up to 40 recently closed ones with at least 20 organisation submissions. Feedback is capped at 5,000 rows per publication; five closed consultations are partial and say so.
  • Matching is similarity plus one model judgment, not a guarantee. The uncertain band is a prompt to check, not a filter.
  • Quotes marked "machine translation by the Commission" come from the English translation the portal publishes next to a submission written in another language. The original language is named; the original wording can differ.
  • Stakeholder types come from the portal's own declaration field and register categories from the register. The model never assigns a category.
  • Meeting subjects are a few words ("EU environmental priorities"). A similarity threshold of 0.45 keeps the list useful, not exact.
  • Scanned PDFs have no text layer and are not OCRed; such submissions appear as "without readable text".
  • Summaries, stances and match reasons are model output. They are cached per publication and shared, so one organisation's page may show a summary written days earlier.
  • Citizens: no name, no text and no register number of an EU or non-EU citizen row is stored. A database constraint makes it impossible to insert one; they are counted and nothing else.
  • Small firms: a submitter with no register number that declared itself a micro company is shown as "Micro company" and its country, because such a name is often a person's name. Long numbers in organisation names (tax or company numbers) are masked.

Sources

  • EU Transparency Register, full XML export (daily).
  • Have Your Say: initiative listing and detail, feedback rows, Commission documents, through the portal's JSON backend, one request per second.
  • Commission meetings with interest representatives, 2024 to 2029 (XLSX).

Stack: Python, Postgres with pgvector, Redis and Celery, LangGraph with a Postgres checkpointer, FastAPI with Jinja2 and HTMX, local embeddings (fastembed). Models: Gemini Flash for the bulk work and the rerank, with a deterministic stand-in for the tests.
