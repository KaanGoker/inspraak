Cloud and AI Development Act

Proposal for a REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL establishing a framework of measures for strengthening Europe’s cloud and AI ecosystem (Cloud and AI Development Act)

Why it fits: Strengthening Europe's cloud and AI ecosystem directly targets DIGITALEUROPE's core mission representing the digital technology industry. (model judgment)

Deadline 11 Nov 2026 match portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission proposes the Cloud and AI Development Act to establish a framework of measures aimed at strengthening Europe's cloud and artificial intelligence ecosystem. The initiative focuses on three pillars, which include research and innovation for greening infrastructure, industrial investments to triple EU data processing capacity, and actions to increase secure data processing capacities for cloud providers in the EU. Ultimately, the proposal seeks to address infrastructure gaps, reduce critical dependencies on non-European providers, and enhance the Union's digital sovereignty and competitiveness.

  • Addresses the EU gap in cloud and AI infrastructure capacity through three distinct pillars.
  • Promotes research and innovation to accelerate sustainable cloud and AI computing technologies.
  • Aims to triple the EU data processing capacity over the next 5 to 7 years via industrial investments.
  • Introduces measures to increase secure data processing capacities for high-critical use cases.
  • Establishes common criteria for sovereign cloud computing services to reduce extraterritorial dependencies.
  • Promotes open-source solutions and joint public procurement activities across the public sector.

Child Safety Online

Proposal for a REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL EU KIDS ACT - 'EU Keeping Internet Digital Spaces Accountable and Trustworthy'

Why it fits: Regulations on child safety online directly impose design and compliance obligations on member digital platforms and online services. (model judgment)

Deadline 26 Nov 2026 match portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission proposes the EU Kids Act regulation to establish a uniform digital environment for children across the European Union. The proposal introduces harmonized age limits, restricting autonomous account creation on social media and video sharing platforms for children under 15, with parental involvement allowed for older minors. It also sets out security-by-design requirements for online services, video sharing platforms, online games, AI companions, and app stores. Furthermore, the regulation establishes proportionate, privacy-compliant age-verification frameworks and harmonized enforcement mechanisms to prevent market fragmentation.

  • Establishes a single EU-wide digital age limit and restricts autonomous account creation on social media and video sharing platforms for children under 15.
  • Sets out security-by-design requirements for social media, video platforms, online games, AI companions, and app stores.
  • Introduces proportional and privacy-respecting age-verification criteria and safeguards.
  • Defines a structured enforcement framework relying on existing structures with expedited procedures.
  • Builds on existing EU rules such as the Digital Services Act and the Artificial Intelligence Act to prevent regulatory fragmentation in the single market.

Minimum performance standards for data centres in Europe

Establishment of common Union minimum performance standards for data centres located in its territory

Why it fits: Performance standards for data centres regulate critical digital infrastructure operated and built by its member industries. (model judgment)

Same initiative, also open: 26059

Deadline 14 Dec 2026 match portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission is preparing an initiative to establish common Union minimum performance standards for data centres located in its territory. Building on existing reporting and rating schemes under the Energy Efficiency Directive, the proposed regulation aims to phase out unsustainable practices and technologies in new or retrofitted facilities. The initiative seeks to address rising energy and water consumption, limited waste heat reuse, and regulatory fragmentation across Member States. An impact assessment is planned for late 2026, with a legislative proposal anticipated in the second quarter of 2027.

  • Establish common EU minimum performance standards for data centres regarding energy efficiency, water use, waste-heat reuse, and grid services.
  • Form the third step in the EU sustainability framework for data centres, following the 2024 reporting scheme and the 2026 rating scheme.
  • Prevent divergent national approaches and reduce regulatory fragmentation across Member States.
  • Improve sector competitiveness and reduce operational costs by cutting energy and water waste.
  • Support energy system integration by aligning data centre operations with power grid and heat network needs.
  • Prepare an impact assessment in Q4 2026 to support the drafting of the legislative proposal for Q2 2027.

What is asked: The consultation aims to collect in-depth evidence, information, data and feedback from stakeholders on the need for and feasibility of minimum performance standards for data centres in the EU, the most appropriate way to establish them, which indicators should have standards, and what thresholds should be set.

What the proposal says on your topics (1 Commission document; passages closest to the profile)

  1. This sector touches upon two important EU objectives, namely the Union’s technological sovereignty and the Union’s energy resilience and security. Expanding computing capacities and increasing the number of data centres on the Union’s territory is essential, but it also poses challenges, not only in terms of increasing energy consumption but also for electricity grids, carbon emissions and environmental resources such as water. In response, the EU’s digital strategy emphasises the need for highly energy-efficient and sustainable data centres. As highlighted by the Draghi report on EU competitiveness, using energy more efficiently makes it possible to cut costs, which is vital for the competitiveness of European industries. The European Commission has launched a public consultation to evaluate Regulation (EU) 2021/821 on the control of exports, brokering, technical assistance, transit, and transfer of dual-use items. Pursuant to Article 26(4) of the Regulation, the evaluation aims to determine whether the rules have achieved their objectives and remain effective. Furthermore, the evaluation examines potential ways to reduce administrative burdens for businesses.

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission is consulting on the role of European partnerships implemented as joint undertakings under the Horizon Europe framework programme for 2028-2034. The initiative proposes simplifying the instrument by streamlining implementation modes and applying a portfolio approach to identify a limited set of coherent partnerships. These partnerships will feature objectives closely aligned with the Competitiveness Fund. Furthermore, the proposal suggests establishing joint undertakings under Article 187 TFEU through a single basic act with harmonised rules.

  • Streamline implementation modes for European partnerships under Horizon Europe 2028-2034
  • Use a portfolio approach to identify a limited set of coherent partnerships
  • Align partnership objectives closely with the Competitiveness Fund
  • Establish joint undertakings under Article 187 TFEU with clear added value
  • Implement a single basic act with harmonised rules for joint undertakings
