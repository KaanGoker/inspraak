EU emissions trading system for maritime, aviation and stationary installations, and market stability reserve - review

Proposal for a DIRECTIVE OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL amending Directive 2003/87/EC and Decision (EU) 2015/1814 as regards driving competitiveness and cost-effective decarbonisation

Why it fits: EU ETS review covering aviation and maritime emissions sits at the very core of T&E transport decarbonisation mission. (model judgment)

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission has published an impact assessment and legislative review concerning Directive 2003/87/EC and Decision (EU) 2015/1814 on the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) and the Market Stability Reserve. This initiative serves as the first part of a broad post-2030 framework package aimed at driving competitiveness and cost-effective decarbonisation. It evaluates how stationary installations, aviation, and maritime sectors can deliver necessary emission reductions while ensuring carbon leakage protection and orderly market functioning. The review addresses the shrinking supply of allowances and adapts the system to align with intermediate 2040 targets and 2050 climate neutrality goals.

  • Reviews the EU ETS and the Market Stability Reserve to prepare for post-2030 climate targets.
  • Assesses pathways toward the 2050 climate neutrality goal and the intermediate 2040 target.
  • Focuses on industrial sectors, maritime transport, and aviation to ensure cost-effective decarbonisation.
  • Examines carbon leakage protection, free allocation benchmarks, and the funding transition through EU revenues.
  • Analyzes market functioning and allowance supply adjustments to prevent trading frictions and price volatility.
  • Accompanies related proposals simplifying monitoring, reporting, and verification regulations.

Minimum values for the electrochemical performance and durability of rechargeable industrial batteries

COMMISSION DELEGATED REGULATION (EU) …/... supplementing and amending Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 of the European Parliament and of the Council by laying down minimum values for the electrochemical performance and durability of rechargeable industrial batteries, technical and safety parameters, and standards concerning the battery passport

Why it fits: Performance and durability standards for industrial batteries directly affect battery storage and clean energy mobility supply chains. (model judgment)

Deadline 27 Oct 2026 match portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

This draft Commission Delegated Regulation supplements Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 by establishing minimum values for the electrochemical performance and durability of rechargeable industrial batteries with a capacity greater than 2 kWh. It sets specific thresholds for parameters such as capacity fade, expected lifetime in cycles or years, and energy round trip efficiency across different battery categories including residential storage, repetitive energy supply, and on-demand batteries. The act also outlines measurement and calculation methods, stress factor determinations, and rules for batteries that have undergone preparation for re-use, repurposing, or remanufacturing. Products designed for defence purposes are excluded from the scope of these requirements.

  • Establishes minimum performance and durability values for rechargeable industrial batteries exceeding 2 kWh capacity, excluding those with exclusively external storage or for defence purposes.
  • Sets specific criteria for residential battery energy storage systems, covering capacity fade limits, expected lifetime in cycles, and a minimum energy round trip efficiency greater than 93%.
  • Defines requirements for repetitive energy supply batteries and lead-based variants, detailing distinct capacity fade thresholds and cycle life expectations adjusted by stress factors.
  • Sets durability rules for on-demand batteries, requiring an expected lifetime in years multiplied by a stress factor to exceed 10 years.
  • Specifies measurement and calculation methods for capacity fade, expected lifetime, and round trip efficiency, incorporating stress factors related to temperature, depth of discharge, and C-rates.
  • Provides rules for batteries that have been subjected to preparation for re-use, repurposing, or remanufacturing, including compliance pathways based on previous use and operational data.

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission proposes an omnibus regulation to simplify and improve the use of digital options under Regulation (EU) 2017/1369 for energy labelling and Regulation (EU) 2020/740 for tyre labelling. The initiative aims to reduce administrative burdens and lower compliance costs for businesses while maintaining consumer protection and product transparency. It introduces targeted amendments to make label delivery, display rules, and database registration more effective. The proposal builds on previous implementation reports, stakeholder consultations, and an impact assessment.

  • Amends Regulation (EU) 2017/1369 on energy labelling and Regulation (EU) 2020/740 on tyre labelling.
  • Aims to reduce administrative costs and burdens for businesses.
  • Simplifies rules for complying with energy and tyre label delivery and display.
  • Facilitates simpler registration in the European Product Registry for Energy Labelling (EPREL).
  • Improves surveillance and compliance within the regulatory framework.
  • Allows for the possibility of amending the tyre label via delegated acts.

What is asked: The Commission invites feedback on the proposed targeted amendments to simplify energy and tyre labelling rules, improve digital options, and reduce administrative burdens for businesses while maintaining effective consumer information.

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

This Commission implementing decision lays down a revised common format for national air pollution control programmes under Directive (EU) 2016/2284. It aims to simplify and clarify reporting requirements, improve data comparability, and support Member States in planning measures to meet national emission reduction commitments. The decision repeals the previous Implementing Decision (EU) 2018/1522 and introduces a 12-month transitional period allowing Member States to use either the old or the new format. The annex details specific reporting sections covering general information, policy frameworks, emission inventories, air quality status, and projected impacts.

  • Establishes a revised common format for initial and updated National Air Pollution Control Programmes (NAPCPs).
  • Repeals Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2018/1522.
  • Grants a 12-month transitional period where Member States can use either the previous or the new format.
  • Requires structured reporting on general administrative information, air quality policy frameworks, current emission and air quality situations, transboundary impacts, and adopted policies and measures.
  • Mandates quantitative projections of future emissions for key pollutants including SO2, NOx, NMVOC, NH3, and PM2.5.
