Profile as registered

Goals
Corporate Europe Observatory (CEO) is a research and campaign group working to expose and challenge the privileged access and influence enjoyed by corporations and their lobby groups in EU policy making. Corporate Europe Observatory is strongly committed to transparency about EU lobbying. CEO believes that the Transparency Register fails basic transparency standards and does not yet live up to its name. We support a legally-binding register, stronger safeguards against inaccurate reporting, and the publication of the names of all individual interest representatives. We regret that the 2021 register revamp no longer requires NGOs and think tanks to submit a figure for an annual lobby spend. We will continue to provide such a figure in the notes below. We also regret that the names of individual lobbyists can no longer be provided.

Proposal for a DIRECTIVE OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL amending Directive 2003/87/EC and Decision (EU) 2015/1814 as regards driving competitiveness and cost-effective decarbonisation

Why it fits: Corporate lobbying on EU carbon markets and climate policy is a primary campaign focus for CEO. (model judgment; similarity 0.66)

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission has published an impact assessment and legislative review concerning Directive 2003/87/EC and Decision (EU) 2015/1814 on the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) and the Market Stability Reserve. This initiative serves as the first part of a broad post-2030 framework package aimed at driving competitiveness and cost-effective decarbonisation. It evaluates how stationary installations, aviation, and maritime sectors can deliver necessary emission reductions while ensuring carbon leakage protection and orderly market functioning. The review addresses the shrinking supply of allowances and adapts the system to align with intermediate 2040 targets and 2050 climate neutrality goals.

  • Reviews the EU ETS and the Market Stability Reserve to prepare for post-2030 climate targets.
  • Assesses pathways toward the 2050 climate neutrality goal and the intermediate 2040 target.
  • Focuses on industrial sectors, maritime transport, and aviation to ensure cost-effective decarbonisation.
  • Examines carbon leakage protection, free allocation benchmarks, and the funding transition through EU revenues.
  • Analyzes market functioning and allowance supply adjustments to prevent trading frictions and price volatility.
  • Accompanies related proposals simplifying monitoring, reporting, and verification regulations.

The European Commission is consulting on the role of European partnerships implemented as joint undertakings under the Horizon Europe framework programme for 2028-2034. Proposal for a COUNCIL DIRECTIVE amending Directives 2003/49/EC, 2009/133/EC, 2011/96/EU, (EU) 2016/1164, (EU)2017/1852, (EU) 2025/50 as regards the simplification of the Union framework on direct taxation and supporting growth and competitiveness of the EU

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The proposal introduces a Council Directive, known as the Omnibus on Taxation, which amends six existing directives in the field of direct taxation to simplify the EU corporate tax framework and support business growth and competitiveness. It aims to reduce administrative and compliance burdens for cross-border activities while maintaining high standards of protection against tax avoidance and abusive practices. The initiative aligns existing rules with international tax developments, such as the Pillar Two global minimum tax, and addresses procedural and legal obstacles in areas like withholding taxes and corporate reorganisations. Overall, the measures seek to improve legal certainty and ensure the coherent functioning of the internal market without lowering tax standards.

  • Amends Directives 2003/49/EC, 2009/133/EC, 2011/96/EU, (EU) 2016/1164, (EU) 2017/1852, and (EU) 2025/50.
  • Simplifies the Union framework on direct taxation to reduce compliance costs for businesses.
  • Eliminates overlapping provisions and addresses practical issues arising from the interaction with the Pillar Two Directive.
  • Removes procedural barriers and legal uncertainty linked to withholding tax relief.
  • Maintains existing safeguards and high levels of protection against tax avoidance and evasion.

What is asked: Respondents expressed their views on simplifying existing EU tax rules to improve the functioning of the internal market and enhance Europe's attractiveness for investment and business.

The European Commission proposes a revision of EU public procurement rules through a new regulation that repeals the existing 2014 directives on public procurement, utilities, and concessions. The initiative aims to modernize and simplify rules, reduce administrative burdens, and enable preferences for European products in strategic sectors to secure supply chains and add EU value. It also amends multiple sector-specific regulations and directives containing procurement provisions to improve coherence and transparency. Furthermore, the proposal addresses high administrative burdens and legal uncertainty identified in previous evaluations regarding public buyers and businesses.

  • Revises and replaces Directives 2014/23/EU, 2014/24/EU, and 2014/25/EU with a single Public Procurement Act regulation.
  • Enables preference for European products in strategic sectors to ensure security of supply and EU added value.
  • Aims to modernize and simplify rules, specifically considering EU start-ups and innovators.
  • Amends various sector-specific regulations and directives that contain public procurement provisions.
  • Addresses administrative burdens and legal uncertainty that currently affect public buyers and businesses, including SMEs.

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission is preparing a diversification package, including a new trade instrument and supporting measures, to address critical strategic dependencies on single supplier countries. The initiative aims to accelerate supply chain resilience and reduce economic security risks in critical sectors where non-EU countries may use dependencies as leverage. It combines trade policy measures based on Article 207 TFEU with single market measures based on Article 114 TFEU to establish a comprehensive regulatory framework. This call for evidence invites public and stakeholder feedback to inform the Commission's impact assessment and future legislative work.

  • Targets concentrated critical dependencies on dominant supplier countries for strategic sectors.
  • Proposes a new trade diversification instrument complemented by regulatory and financial support measures.
  • Combines trade policy measures under Article 207 TFEU and Single Market measures under Article 114 TFEU.
  • Seeks to address market gaps such as the lack of appropriate price signals and incentives for business de-risking.
  • Aims to protect the EU from supply chain disruptions, geopolitical pressure, and market volatility.

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide views on the prioritisation of actions to accelerate supply diversification, the legal framework and incentives needed to support business resilience, and any additional diversification measures the EU should consider.
