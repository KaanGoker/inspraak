About ETUC

As the organisation describes itself in the EU Transparency Register. This is what the matching reads.

Goals
Set up in 1973, the ETUC comprises 93 national trade union confederations in 41 countries, plus 10 European trade union federations. The ETUC speaks with a single voice on behalf of European workers to have a stronger say in EU decision-making and aims to ensure that the EU is not just a single market for goods and services, but is also a Social Europe which delivers better working and living conditions and promotes collective bargaining and social dialogue, as the most successful way for working people to create a fair and sustainable future for themselves, their families and their communities. The ETUC defends the rights and interests of working people and trade unions at European level in different areas and topics and defends fundamental social values such as solidarity, equality, democracy, social justice and cohesion.

Revision of EU rules on public procurement until 30 Nov 2026 good fit Public procurement rules are a major priority for trade unions seeking to enforce social clauses, collective bargaining standards, and worker protections in public contracts.

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission proposes a revision of EU public procurement rules through a new regulation that repeals the existing 2014 directives on public procurement, utilities, and concessions. The initiative aims to modernize and simplify rules, reduce administrative burdens, and enable preferences for European products in strategic sectors to secure supply chains and add EU value. It also amends multiple sector-specific regulations and directives containing procurement provisions to improve coherence and transparency. Furthermore, the proposal addresses high administrative burdens and legal uncertainty identified in previous evaluations regarding public buyers and businesses.

  • Revises and replaces Directives 2014/23/EU, 2014/24/EU, and 2014/25/EU with a single Public Procurement Act regulation.
  • Enables preference for European products in strategic sectors to ensure security of supply and EU added value.
  • Aims to modernize and simplify rules, specifically considering EU start-ups and innovators.
  • Amends various sector-specific regulations and directives that contain public procurement provisions.
  • Addresses administrative burdens and legal uncertainty that currently affect public buyers and businesses, including SMEs.

Omnibus on taxation until 5 Nov 2026 good fit Corporate taxation and anti-abuse rules directly concern the financing of social models and fair economic distribution defended by trade unions.

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The proposal introduces a Council Directive, known as the Omnibus on Taxation, which amends six existing directives in the field of direct taxation to simplify the EU corporate tax framework and support business growth and competitiveness. It aims to reduce administrative and compliance burdens for cross-border activities while maintaining high standards of protection against tax avoidance and abusive practices. The initiative aligns existing rules with international tax developments, such as the Pillar Two global minimum tax, and addresses procedural and legal obstacles in areas like withholding taxes and corporate reorganisations. Overall, the measures seek to improve legal certainty and ensure the coherent functioning of the internal market without lowering tax standards.

  • Amends Directives 2003/49/EC, 2009/133/EC, 2011/96/EU, (EU) 2016/1164, (EU) 2017/1852, and (EU) 2025/50.
  • Simplifies the Union framework on direct taxation to reduce compliance costs for businesses.
  • Eliminates overlapping provisions and addresses practical issues arising from the interaction with the Pillar Two Directive.
  • Removes procedural barriers and legal uncertainty linked to withholding tax relief.
  • Maintains existing safeguards and high levels of protection against tax avoidance and evasion.

What is asked: Respondents expressed their views on simplifying existing EU tax rules to improve the functioning of the internal market and enhance Europe's attractiveness for investment and business.

Review of the Brussels Ia Regulation until 24 Nov 2026 possible fit Jurisdiction and enforcement of civil judgments can touch cross-border employment litigation, though the initiative primarily targets commercial litigation.

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission has launched an initiative to review Regulation (EU) No 1215/2012 on jurisdiction and the recognition and enforcement of judgments in civil and commercial matters. The initiative aims to simplify, clarify, and modernize the existing rules to reduce litigation costs, address legal fragmentation, and support the EU single market and competitiveness. It will be supported by a back-to-back evaluation and impact assessment, with a legislative proposal planned for the fourth quarter of 2027. The review addresses challenges identified in an application report, stakeholder consultations, and Court of Justice case law.

  • Review and potential legislative amendment of Regulation (EU) No 1215/2012.
  • Address inconsistent jurisdiction rules, particularly for defendants outside the EU.
  • Improve procedural tools for collective redress and assess aspects relevant to strategic lawsuits against public participation (SLAPs).
  • Evaluate parallel cross-border enforcement mechanisms under Regulation (EC) 805/2004.
  • Consider policy options ranging from the baseline scenario to targeted clarification, simplification, and modernization.
  • Planned adoption of the initiative in the fourth quarter of 2027.

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide views on the Commission's understanding of the problem and possible solutions, and to give any relevant information including on the possible impacts of the different options.

Cloud and AI Development Act until 11 Nov 2026 possible fit Digital sovereignty and AI infrastructure investments affect the broader European economy and workforce, but this proposal focuses primarily on technical industrial capacity.

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission proposes the Cloud and AI Development Act to establish a framework of measures aimed at strengthening Europe's cloud and artificial intelligence ecosystem. The initiative focuses on three pillars, which include research and innovation for greening infrastructure, industrial investments to triple EU data processing capacity, and actions to increase secure data processing capacities for cloud providers in the EU. Ultimately, the proposal seeks to address infrastructure gaps, reduce critical dependencies on non-European providers, and enhance the Union's digital sovereignty and competitiveness.

  • Addresses the EU gap in cloud and AI infrastructure capacity through three distinct pillars.
  • Promotes research and innovation to accelerate sustainable cloud and AI computing technologies.
  • Aims to triple the EU data processing capacity over the next 5 to 7 years via industrial investments.
  • Introduces measures to increase secure data processing capacities for high-critical use cases.
  • Establishes common criteria for sovereign cloud computing services to reduce extraterritorial dependencies.
  • Promotes open-source solutions and joint public procurement activities across the public sector.

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission is conducting a review of the scope, functioning, and efficiency of Regulation (EU) 2022/1031 on the International Procurement Instrument (IPI). The review evaluates the implementation of the regulation, including investigation processes, consultations, thresholds, exceptions, and the first adopted IPI measure regarding medical devices from China. It aims to assess the instrument's effectiveness in tackling procurement barriers in non-EU countries and ensuring a level playing field for EU operators. The findings will be presented in a report to the European Parliament and the Council by August 2027.

  • Review of the scope, functioning, and efficiency of the IPI Regulation pursuant to Article 14.
  • Assessment of investigation procedures, third-country consultations, thresholds, and types of measures.
  • Evaluation of the application and implementation of the first IPI measure concerning Chinese medical devices.
  • Analysis of the efficiency of online complaint tools and rules for determining origin.
  • Execution of a 12-week online public consultation through the Have your say portal.
  • Publication of a factual summary of consultation results and an implementation report to the European Parliament and the Council.

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide views and evidence on the implementation, scope, functioning, and efficiency of the IPI Regulation in tackling non-EU procurement barriers, achieving market openness, and ensuring a level playing field, as well as the application of specific measures adopted under the Regulation.
