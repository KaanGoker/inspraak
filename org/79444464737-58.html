List of non-OECD countries that are authorized to import certain non-hazardous waste from the EU

COMMISSION DELEGATED REGULATION (EU) …/... supplementing Regulation (EU) 2024/1157 of the European Parliament and of the Council by establishing a list of countries to which the OECD Decision does not apply and to which exports of certain non-hazardous wastes and mixtures of non-hazardous wastes from the Union for recovery are authorised

Why it fits: Exporting EU waste to non OECD countries directly impacts environmental justice and partner communities in Africa, Asia, and Latin America. (model judgment)

Deadline 16 Oct 2026 match portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

This draft Commission Delegated Regulation establishes a list of non-OECD countries and territories to which exports of certain non-hazardous wastes and mixtures of non-hazardous wastes from the EU for recovery are authorized. It implements provisions from Regulation (EU) 2024/1157 on shipments of waste, which generally bans non-hazardous waste exports to non-OECD countries starting on 21 May 2027. Thirty-two non-OECD countries or territories submitted requests to the Commission to be included in this authorized list. The Commission assessed these requests to determine whether the applicants demonstrated an ability to manage the concerned waste in an environmentally sound manner equivalent to EU requirements.

  • Supplements Regulation (EU) 2024/1157 by establishing a list of non-OECD countries authorized to receive certain non-hazardous waste for recovery from the EU.
  • Responds to requests submitted by thirty-two non-OECD countries and territories aiming to continue importing specific EU waste streams after the general ban enters into force on 21 May 2027.
  • Bases country inclusion on an assessment of whether the applicant has implemented measures to ensure environmentally sound management equivalent to EU standards.
  • Excludes certain waste streams, such as ferrous and non-ferrous metal waste, for specific applicant countries due to environmental and health risk profiles.

What is asked: Respondents are invited to provide feedback on the draft delegated act during its publication period on the Better Regulation Portal and through the World Trade Organization Committee on Technical Barriers to Trade.

"Diversify EU" instrument

Commission Proposal for a Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council establishing diversification instrument to address critical strategic dependencies that expose the Union to economic security risks

Why it fits: EU trade dependency instruments and global supply chain policies affect trade relations and economic conditions in partner countries. (model judgment)

Deadline 27 Oct 2026 match portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission is preparing a diversification package, including a new trade instrument and supporting measures, to address critical strategic dependencies on single supplier countries. The initiative aims to accelerate supply chain resilience and reduce economic security risks in critical sectors where non-EU countries may use dependencies as leverage. It combines trade policy measures based on Article 207 TFEU with single market measures based on Article 114 TFEU to establish a comprehensive regulatory framework. This call for evidence invites public and stakeholder feedback to inform the Commission's impact assessment and future legislative work.

  • Targets concentrated critical dependencies on dominant supplier countries for strategic sectors.
  • Proposes a new trade diversification instrument complemented by regulatory and financial support measures.
  • Combines trade policy measures under Article 207 TFEU and Single Market measures under Article 114 TFEU.
  • Seeks to address market gaps such as the lack of appropriate price signals and incentives for business de-risking.
  • Aims to protect the EU from supply chain disruptions, geopolitical pressure, and market volatility.

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide views on the prioritisation of actions to accelerate supply diversification, the legal framework and incentives needed to support business resilience, and any additional diversification measures the EU should consider.

Why it fits: Global climate justice and emission reduction efforts are relevant to solidarity groups although the EU ETS mechanism is primarily European. (model judgment)

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission has published an impact assessment and legislative review concerning Directive 2003/87/EC and Decision (EU) 2015/1814 on the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) and the Market Stability Reserve. This initiative serves as the first part of a broad post-2030 framework package aimed at driving competitiveness and cost-effective decarbonisation. It evaluates how stationary installations, aviation, and maritime sectors can deliver necessary emission reductions while ensuring carbon leakage protection and orderly market functioning. The review addresses the shrinking supply of allowances and adapts the system to align with intermediate 2040 targets and 2050 climate neutrality goals.

  • Reviews the EU ETS and the Market Stability Reserve to prepare for post-2030 climate targets.
  • Assesses pathways toward the 2050 climate neutrality goal and the intermediate 2040 target.
  • Focuses on industrial sectors, maritime transport, and aviation to ensure cost-effective decarbonisation.
  • Examines carbon leakage protection, free allocation benchmarks, and the funding transition through EU revenues.
  • Analyzes market functioning and allowance supply adjustments to prevent trading frictions and price volatility.
  • Accompanies related proposals simplifying monitoring, reporting, and verification regulations.

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission has launched a public consultation to evaluate Regulation (EU) 2021/821 on the control of exports, brokering, technical assistance, transit, and transfer of dual-use items. Pursuant to Article 26(4) of the Regulation, the evaluation aims to determine whether the rules have achieved their objectives and remain effective. It also assesses the legislation in light of the evolving global geopolitical landscape and new security threats. Furthermore, the evaluation examines potential ways to reduce administrative burdens for businesses.

  • Evaluation of Regulation (EU) 2021/821 on dual-use items
  • Assessment of whether the Regulation has achieved its objectives
  • Review of relevance in view of the evolving global geopolitical landscape
  • Analysis of new threats requiring regulatory adjustments
  • Identification of ways to reduce administrative burden for businesses

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide feedback on the performance and relevance of the Dual-Use Regulation, its ability to address new threats in the evolving geopolitical landscape, and ways to reduce administrative burdens for businesses.
