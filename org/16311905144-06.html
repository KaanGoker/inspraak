Child Safety Online until 26 Nov 2026 good fit Online child safety rules including age verification and platform accountability are core topics for digital civil liberties and privacy.

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission proposes the EU Kids Act regulation to establish a uniform digital environment for children across the European Union. The proposal introduces harmonized age limits, restricting autonomous account creation on social media and video sharing platforms for children under 15, with parental involvement allowed for older minors. It also sets out security-by-design requirements for online services, video sharing platforms, online games, AI companions, and app stores. Furthermore, the regulation establishes proportionate, privacy-compliant age-verification frameworks and harmonized enforcement mechanisms to prevent market fragmentation.

  • Establishes a single EU-wide digital age limit and restricts autonomous account creation on social media and video sharing platforms for children under 15.
  • Sets out security-by-design requirements for social media, video platforms, online games, AI companions, and app stores.
  • Introduces proportional and privacy-respecting age-verification criteria and safeguards.
  • Defines a structured enforcement framework relying on existing structures with expedited procedures.
  • Builds on existing EU rules such as the Digital Services Act and the Artificial Intelligence Act to prevent regulatory fragmentation in the single market.

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission has proposed a new regulation on the European Union Agency for Law Enforcement Cooperation (Europol) to reinforce its operational role in combating serious cross-border crime and terrorism. The initiative replaces Regulation (EU) 2016/794, updates Europol's mandate to address a changing security landscape, and provides for increased resources and staff. It also establishes structured cooperation between Europol and eu-LISA for information management and ICT services while ensuring full alignment with EU data protection frameworks.

  • Replaces Regulation (EU) 2016/794 to establish a revised mandate for Europol.
  • Aims to enhance Europol's operational capabilities against online and offline serious crime and terrorism.
  • Establishes structured cooperation between Europol and eu-LISA for information systems and communication infrastructure.
  • Allocates an estimated EUR 1.053 billion and 910 additional staff posts over the 2028-2034 MFF.
  • Maintains alignment with the EU data protection framework and fundamental rights standards.

Cloud and AI Development Act until 11 Nov 2026 good fit EU cloud infrastructure and AI ecosystem regulation directly implicates digital rights, technological accountability, and data processing governance.

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission proposes the Cloud and AI Development Act to establish a framework of measures aimed at strengthening Europe's cloud and artificial intelligence ecosystem. The initiative focuses on three pillars, which include research and innovation for greening infrastructure, industrial investments to triple EU data processing capacity, and actions to increase secure data processing capacities for cloud providers in the EU. Ultimately, the proposal seeks to address infrastructure gaps, reduce critical dependencies on non-European providers, and enhance the Union's digital sovereignty and competitiveness.

  • Addresses the EU gap in cloud and AI infrastructure capacity through three distinct pillars.
  • Promotes research and innovation to accelerate sustainable cloud and AI computing technologies.
  • Aims to triple the EU data processing capacity over the next 5 to 7 years via industrial investments.
  • Introduces measures to increase secure data processing capacities for high-critical use cases.
  • Establishes common criteria for sovereign cloud computing services to reduce extraterritorial dependencies.
  • Promotes open-source solutions and joint public procurement activities across the public sector.

Eurojust Regulation Revision until 4 Nov 2026 good fit Expanding Eurojust into cybercrime and analytical data processing raises significant criminal justice and data protection issues relevant to digital rights.

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission proposes a new regulation on the European Union Agency for Criminal Justice Cooperation (Eurojust) to repeal and replace Regulation (EU) 2018/1727. This initiative responds to an evaluation showing that while Eurojust is indispensable, its operations need greater efficiency and a wider mandate to tackle modern cross-border crime. The proposal expands Eurojust's competence into emerging areas such as cybercrime, gender-based violence, and violations of EU restrictive measures, while transforming it into a more proactive analytical hub. It also aims to streamline the agency's internal structure and improve cooperation with partners like Europol and the European Public Prosecutor Office.

  • Repeals and replaces Regulation (EU) 2018/1727 to establish a renewed legal framework for Eurojust.
  • Expands Eurojust's mandate to cover emerging crime areas including cybercrime, gender-based violence, and violations of EU restrictive measures.
  • Transforms Eurojust from a reactive coordination body into a strategic and analytical hub for cross-border judicial cooperation.
  • Standardises National Members' status and powers to improve governance and operational efficiency.
  • Addresses findings from the 2025 evaluation regarding complex decision-making, wide case scope, and slow procedures with third countries.
  • Aligns with related revisions of the Europol Regulation, the European Investigation Order Directive, and the European Union Data Protection Regulation.

What is asked: The Commission sought the views of citizens, Member States, and other relevant stakeholders on Eurojust and the Eurojust Regulation via an online public consultation to inform the impact assessment for the revision of the Eurojust Regulation.

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission has launched a public consultation to establish common Union minimum performance standards for data centres located in its territory. This initiative builds on existing EU rules on energy efficiency that introduced provisions to promote the energy efficiency and sustainability of data centres. The goal is to further improve the environmental performance and sustainability of these facilities across the European Union. Respondents are invited to provide their views on the creation of these mandatory performance standards.

  • Establishment of common Union minimum performance standards for data centres
  • Focus on energy efficiency and sustainability
  • Building upon existing EU energy efficiency rules
  • Targeting data centres located within EU territory
