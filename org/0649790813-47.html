VAT and circular economy until 4 Nov 2026 good fit The initiative explicitly addresses value added tax deduction rules for passenger cars.

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission has launched a call for evidence for an evaluation and impact assessment running in parallel regarding VAT rules and the circular economy. The initiative aims to modernise selected value added tax rules that currently hinder circular business models, sustainable consumption choices, and the single market for secondary raw materials. It focuses on three specific pillars: second-hand goods, the donation versus destruction of viable goods, and VAT deduction rules for passenger cars. A legislative proposal is indicatively planned for the second quarter of 2027.

  • Evaluates and reviews current EU VAT rules to ensure they align with the circular economy and complement the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation and the Circular Economy Act.
  • Examines the VAT treatment of second-hand, refurbished, and remanufactured goods to address lack of deduction rights and complex margin schemes.
  • Addresses economic distortions where destroying viable goods is more advantageous under current VAT rules than donating them.
  • Considers aligning VAT deduction rules for passenger cars with sustainability objectives and zero-emission mobility goals.
  • Assesses policy options including mandatory or discretionary measures on vehicle deductions, harmonised margin schemes, and VAT relief on donations.

What is asked: The Commission asks respondents to provide feedback and evidence on the evaluation of current VAT rules and the impact assessment of potential policy options regarding second-hand goods, the donation or destruction of viable goods, and VAT deduction rules for passenger cars.

Revision of EU rules on public procurement until 30 Nov 2026 good fit Public procurement rules directly impact public purchasing of buses and commercial vehicle fleets.

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission proposes a revision of EU public procurement rules through a new regulation that repeals the existing 2014 directives on public procurement, utilities, and concessions. The initiative aims to modernize and simplify rules, reduce administrative burdens, and enable preferences for European products in strategic sectors to secure supply chains and add EU value. It also amends multiple sector-specific regulations and directives containing procurement provisions to improve coherence and transparency. Furthermore, the proposal addresses high administrative burdens and legal uncertainty identified in previous evaluations regarding public buyers and businesses.

  • Revises and replaces Directives 2014/23/EU, 2014/24/EU, and 2014/25/EU with a single Public Procurement Act regulation.
  • Enables preference for European products in strategic sectors to ensure security of supply and EU added value.
  • Aims to modernize and simplify rules, specifically considering EU start-ups and innovators.
  • Amends various sector-specific regulations and directives that contain public procurement provisions.
  • Addresses administrative burdens and legal uncertainty that currently affect public buyers and businesses, including SMEs.

Minimum performance standards for data centres in Europe until 14 Dec 2026 good fit Minimum performance standards for data centres bear on digital economy and energy costs that ACEA members rely on, though not core automotive policy.

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

Same initiative, also open: 26059

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission is preparing an initiative to establish common Union minimum performance standards for data centres located in its territory. Building on existing reporting and rating schemes under the Energy Efficiency Directive, the proposed regulation aims to phase out unsustainable practices and technologies in new or retrofitted facilities. The initiative seeks to address rising energy and water consumption, limited waste heat reuse, and regulatory fragmentation across Member States. An impact assessment is planned for late 2026, with a legislative proposal anticipated in the second quarter of 2027.

  • Establish common EU minimum performance standards for data centres regarding energy efficiency, water use, waste-heat reuse, and grid services.
  • Form the third step in the EU sustainability framework for data centres, following the 2024 reporting scheme and the 2026 rating scheme.
  • Prevent divergent national approaches and reduce regulatory fragmentation across Member States.
  • Improve sector competitiveness and reduce operational costs by cutting energy and water waste.
  • Support energy system integration by aligning data centre operations with power grid and heat network needs.
  • Prepare an impact assessment in Q4 2026 to support the drafting of the legislative proposal for Q2 2027.

What is asked: The consultation aims to collect in-depth evidence, information, data and feedback from stakeholders on the need for and feasibility of minimum performance standards for data centres in the EU, the most appropriate way to establish them, which indicators should have standards, and what thresholds should be set.

EU emissions trading system for maritime, aviation and stationary installations, and market stability reserve - review until 3 Nov 2026 possible fit This emissions trading review targets stationary installations aviation and maritime rather than road transport.

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

Same initiative, also open: 25760, 25761

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission has published an impact assessment and legislative review concerning Directive 2003/87/EC and Decision (EU) 2015/1814 on the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) and the Market Stability Reserve. This initiative serves as the first part of a broad post-2030 framework package aimed at driving competitiveness and cost-effective decarbonisation. It evaluates how stationary installations, aviation, and maritime sectors can deliver necessary emission reductions while ensuring carbon leakage protection and orderly market functioning. The review addresses the shrinking supply of allowances and adapts the system to align with intermediate 2040 targets and 2050 climate neutrality goals.

  • Reviews the EU ETS and the Market Stability Reserve to prepare for post-2030 climate targets.
  • Assesses pathways toward the 2050 climate neutrality goal and the intermediate 2040 target.
  • Focuses on industrial sectors, maritime transport, and aviation to ensure cost-effective decarbonisation.
  • Examines carbon leakage protection, free allocation benchmarks, and the funding transition through EU revenues.
  • Analyzes market functioning and allowance supply adjustments to prevent trading frictions and price volatility.
  • Accompanies related proposals simplifying monitoring, reporting, and verification regulations.

Proposal for a new Regulation on the European Union Agency for Law Enforcement Cooperation (Europol) until 6 Nov 2026 possible fit Europol's mandate covers serious cross-border crime, which touches ACEA only peripherally, such as vehicle crime and customs cooperation.

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission has proposed a new regulation on the European Union Agency for Law Enforcement Cooperation (Europol) to reinforce its operational role in combating serious cross-border crime and terrorism. The initiative replaces Regulation (EU) 2016/794, updates Europol's mandate to address a changing security landscape, and provides for increased resources and staff. It also establishes structured cooperation between Europol and eu-LISA for information management and ICT services while ensuring full alignment with EU data protection frameworks.

  • Replaces Regulation (EU) 2016/794 to establish a revised mandate for Europol.
  • Aims to enhance Europol's operational capabilities against online and offline serious crime and terrorism.
  • Establishes structured cooperation between Europol and eu-LISA for information systems and communication infrastructure.
  • Allocates an estimated EUR 1.053 billion and 910 additional staff posts over the 2028-2034 MFF.
  • Maintains alignment with the EU data protection framework and fundamental rights standards.
