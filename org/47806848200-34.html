Zero Waste Europe ZWE

Non-governmental organisations, platforms and networks and similar. Head office Brussels, Belgium. Register ID 47806848200-34, last updated 4 Jun 2026.

Profile as registered

Goals
Zero Waste Europe (ZWE) is the European network of communities, local leaders, experts, and change agents working towards a better use of resources and the elimination of waste in our society. We advocate for sustainable systems; for the redesign of our relationship with resources; and for a global shift towards environmental justice, accelerating a just transition towards zero waste for the benefit of people and the planet. ZWE is participating in the international organisation Global Alliance for Incinerator Alternatives (GAIA) and the Zero Waste International Alliance (ZWIA). Read about ZWE's Strategic Framework 2025-2030 here: https://zerowasteeurope.eu/library/zwe-strategic-framework-2025-2030/

We foster the transition to a zero waste Europe by creating the enabling conditions in terms of policy; regulations; financial drivers; implementing zero waste solutions on the ground; and organising and mobilising other groups inside and outside the organisation, initiating coalitions to further promote our narrative. ZWE is involved in the transposition of waste directives, including waste package (waste framework directive, the Landfill directive, ecodesign) and single-use plastics legislation (such as the revision of the Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive and the Sustainable Products initiative) but also product, energy and toxics & health policies (FCM regulation, Chemicals Strategy for Sustainability). ZWE is also working on climate policies such as EU 2050 climate strategy, Renewable Energy Directive, EU Emissions Trading Scheme (EU ETS) and EU Sustainable Finance Taxonomies. ZWE works on other issues as a member of the international network GAIA but these activities don't fall under the register.

Early warning report on waste 2027

REPORT FROM THE COMMISSION TO THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT, THE COUNCIL, THE EUROPEAN ECONOMIC AND SOCIAL COMMITTEE AND THE COMMITTEE OF THE REGIONS identifying Member States at risk of not meeting the 2030 targets for the reduction of packaging waste and food waste, the preparing for re-use and recycling target for municipal waste, the recycling target for packaging waste and the 2035 municipal waste landfilling reduction target

Why it fits: Tracking progress on municipal recycling alongside packaging and food waste reduction is the core focus of Zero Waste Europe. (model judgment)

Deadline 26 Oct 2026 match portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission is preparing the 2027 early warning report to assess Member States progress towards 2030 targets for municipal waste recycling, packaging waste reduction and recycling, and food waste reduction, alongside the 2035 municipal waste landfilling reduction target. The report identifies countries at risk of missing these targets and provides tailored recommendations and best practices to improve their performance. This initiative fulfils a legal obligation under EU waste directives without introducing new policy objectives or requiring an impact assessment. It focuses on evaluating national policies and implementation measures that directly impact waste prevention, separate collection, sorting, and recycling.

  • Assess progress towards 2030 waste reduction and recycling targets and 2035 landfill reduction targets.
  • Identify Member States at risk of not meeting the established targets.
  • Provide general and country-specific recommendations alongside best practice sharing.
  • Fulfill legal reporting obligations under the Waste Framework Directive, Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive, and Landfill Directive.
  • Incorporate data and methodology developed by the European Environment Agency and its European Topic Centre.
  • Include dedicated country-specific annexes for Member States identified at risk.

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide views on the Commission's understanding of the problem and possible solutions, identify implementation challenges present in Member States, suggest measures to improve waste management performance, and provide input on what general and country-specific recommendations should be made.

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

This draft Commission Delegated Regulation establishes a list of non-OECD countries and territories to which exports of certain non-hazardous wastes and mixtures of non-hazardous wastes from the EU for recovery are authorized. It implements provisions from Regulation (EU) 2024/1157 on shipments of waste, which generally bans non-hazardous waste exports to non-OECD countries starting on 21 May 2027. Thirty-two non-OECD countries or territories submitted requests to the Commission to be included in this authorized list. The Commission assessed these requests to determine whether the applicants demonstrated an ability to manage the concerned waste in an environmentally sound manner equivalent to EU requirements.

  • Supplements Regulation (EU) 2024/1157 by establishing a list of non-OECD countries authorized to receive certain non-hazardous waste for recovery from the EU.
  • Responds to requests submitted by thirty-two non-OECD countries and territories aiming to continue importing specific EU waste streams after the general ban enters into force on 21 May 2027.
  • Bases country inclusion on an assessment of whether the applicant has implemented measures to ensure environmentally sound management equivalent to EU standards.
  • Excludes certain waste streams, such as ferrous and non-ferrous metal waste, for specific applicant countries due to environmental and health risk profiles.

What is asked: Respondents are invited to provide feedback on the draft delegated act during its publication period on the Better Regulation Portal and through the World Trade Organization Committee on Technical Barriers to Trade.

VAT and circular economy

Proposal for a Council Directive amending Directive 2006/112/EC as regards the support of the circular economy

Why it fits: Tax rules addressing the circular economy and halting the destruction of unsold goods align directly with the organisation's core mission. (model judgment)

Same initiative, also open: 25912

Deadline 4 Nov 2026 match portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission has launched a call for evidence for an evaluation and impact assessment running in parallel regarding VAT rules and the circular economy. The initiative aims to modernise selected value added tax rules that currently hinder circular business models, sustainable consumption choices, and the single market for secondary raw materials. It focuses on three specific pillars: second-hand goods, the donation versus destruction of viable goods, and VAT deduction rules for passenger cars. A legislative proposal is indicatively planned for the second quarter of 2027.

  • Evaluates and reviews current EU VAT rules to ensure they align with the circular economy and complement the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation and the Circular Economy Act.
  • Examines the VAT treatment of second-hand, refurbished, and remanufactured goods to address lack of deduction rights and complex margin schemes.
  • Addresses economic distortions where destroying viable goods is more advantageous under current VAT rules than donating them.
  • Considers aligning VAT deduction rules for passenger cars with sustainability objectives and zero-emission mobility goals.
  • Assesses policy options including mandatory or discretionary measures on vehicle deductions, harmonised margin schemes, and VAT relief on donations.

What is asked: The Commission asks respondents to provide feedback and evidence on the evaluation of current VAT rules and the impact assessment of potential policy options regarding second-hand goods, the donation or destruction of viable goods, and VAT deduction rules for passenger cars.
