EU emissions trading system for maritime, aviation and stationary installations, and market stability reserve - review

Proposal for a DIRECTIVE OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL amending Directive 2003/87/EC and Decision (EU) 2015/1814 as regards driving competitiveness and cost-effective decarbonisation

Why it fits: The EU Emissions Trading System review directly addresses core climate action and decarbonisation policy. (model judgment; similarity 0.63)

Same initiative, also open: 25760, 25761

Deadline 3 Nov 2026 match, relevance 1.00 portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission has published an impact assessment and legislative review concerning Directive 2003/87/EC and Decision (EU) 2015/1814 on the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) and the Market Stability Reserve. This initiative serves as the first part of a broad post-2030 framework package aimed at driving competitiveness and cost-effective decarbonisation. It evaluates how stationary installations, aviation, and maritime sectors can deliver necessary emission reductions while ensuring carbon leakage protection and orderly market functioning. The review addresses the shrinking supply of allowances and adapts the system to align with intermediate 2040 targets and 2050 climate neutrality goals.

  • Reviews the EU ETS and the Market Stability Reserve to prepare for post-2030 climate targets.
  • Assesses pathways toward the 2050 climate neutrality goal and the intermediate 2040 target.
  • Focuses on industrial sectors, maritime transport, and aviation to ensure cost-effective decarbonisation.
  • Examines carbon leakage protection, free allocation benchmarks, and the funding transition through EU revenues.
  • Analyzes market functioning and allowance supply adjustments to prevent trading frictions and price volatility.
  • Accompanies related proposals simplifying monitoring, reporting, and verification regulations.

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission is preparing the 2027 early warning report to assess Member States progress towards 2030 targets for municipal waste recycling, packaging waste reduction and recycling, and food waste reduction, alongside the 2035 municipal waste landfilling reduction target. The report identifies countries at risk of missing these targets and provides tailored recommendations and best practices to improve their performance. This initiative fulfils a legal obligation under EU waste directives without introducing new policy objectives or requiring an impact assessment. It focuses on evaluating national policies and implementation measures that directly impact waste prevention, separate collection, sorting, and recycling.

  • Assess progress towards 2030 waste reduction and recycling targets and 2035 landfill reduction targets.
  • Identify Member States at risk of not meeting the established targets.
  • Provide general and country-specific recommendations alongside best practice sharing.
  • Fulfill legal reporting obligations under the Waste Framework Directive, Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive, and Landfill Directive.
  • Incorporate data and methodology developed by the European Environment Agency and its European Topic Centre.
  • Include dedicated country-specific annexes for Member States identified at risk.

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide views on the Commission's understanding of the problem and possible solutions, identify implementation challenges present in Member States, suggest measures to improve waste management performance, and provide input on what general and country-specific recommendations should be made.

Why it fits: Integrated Atlantic macro-regional and maritime strategies encompass regional environmental protection and sustainable ocean governance. (model judgment; similarity 0.55)

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission is preparing a new EU Atlantic Maritime and Macro-regional Strategy to be adopted by June 2027, following a request from the European Council. Building on the existing Atlantic Maritime Strategy and Action Plan, the initiative aims to provide a broader, integrated territorial framework to address shared challenges and opportunities across the Atlantic region. It seeks to improve policy coordination, foster innovative solutions, strengthen multi-level governance, and increase the political weight of Atlantic priorities. The strategy targets the four EU Atlantic Member States and will be established through a non-legislative Communication without an impact assessment.

  • Development of a new EU Atlantic Maritime and Macro-regional Strategy by June 2027.
  • Builds on and integrates the Atlantic Maritime Strategy and the Atlantic Action Plan.
  • Aims to improve policy coordination across EU, national, and regional levels and funding instruments.
  • Restricted in scope to the four EU Atlantic Member States: France, Ireland, Portugal, and Spain.
  • Will incorporate a monitoring framework integrated into the Commission's biennial reports on macro-regional strategies.
  • Takes the form of a non-legislative Communication from the Commission without requiring an impact assessment.

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide views on the Commission's understanding of the problem and possible solutions, and to share any relevant information they may have regarding the Atlantic maritime and macro-regional strategy initiative.

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission has launched a public consultation to evaluate the Balanced Approach Regulation on noise at EU airports. This regulation provides a framework for Member States to manage aviation noise and increase the sustainability of air transport. The evaluation assesses whether the current rules have successfully achieved their objectives and remain fit for purpose. It examines how the framework balances noise mitigation with the economic impact on stakeholders.

  • Evaluates the Balanced Approach Regulation governing noise at EU airports
  • Assesses whether the regulation has achieved its objective
  • Examines if the framework is still fit for purpose
  • Considers the balance between air transport sustainability and economic impacts on stakeholders

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide feedback on the performance, effectiveness, and continued relevance of the Balanced Approach Regulation.

Proposal for a REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL establishing a framework of measures for strengthening Europe’s cloud and AI ecosystem (Cloud and AI Development Act)

Why it fits: The file focuses on digital sovereignty and cloud capacity though it touches peripherally on greening digital infrastructure. (model judgment; similarity 0.59)

Deadline 11 Nov 2026 uncertain relevance, relevance 0.40 portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission proposes the Cloud and AI Development Act to establish a framework of measures aimed at strengthening Europe's cloud and artificial intelligence ecosystem. The initiative focuses on three pillars, which include research and innovation for greening infrastructure, industrial investments to triple EU data processing capacity, and actions to increase secure data processing capacities for cloud providers in the EU. Ultimately, the proposal seeks to address infrastructure gaps, reduce critical dependencies on non-European providers, and enhance the Union's digital sovereignty and competitiveness.

  • Addresses the EU gap in cloud and AI infrastructure capacity through three distinct pillars.
  • Promotes research and innovation to accelerate sustainable cloud and AI computing technologies.
  • Aims to triple the EU data processing capacity over the next 5 to 7 years via industrial investments.
  • Introduces measures to increase secure data processing capacities for high-critical use cases.
  • Establishes common criteria for sovereign cloud computing services to reduce extraterritorial dependencies.
  • Promotes open-source solutions and joint public procurement activities across the public sector.
