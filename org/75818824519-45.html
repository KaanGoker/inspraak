Freezing of meat of domestic ungulates

COMMISSION DELEGATED REGULATION (EU) …/... amending Annex III to Regulation (EC) No 853/2004 of the European Parliament and of the Council as regards hygiene rules on storage conditions of fresh meat of domestic ungulates before freezing

Why it fits: Regulates food hygiene and storage conditions before freezing meat, directly affecting food business operators. (model judgment; similarity 0.61)

Deadline 5 Oct 2026 match, relevance 0.95 portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission proposes to amend Annex III to Regulation (EC) No 853/2004 to update hygiene rules on the storage conditions of fresh meat of domestic ungulates before freezing. Based on a scientific opinion by EFSA, the draft regulation replaces the general requirement of freezing without undue delay with more specific time and temperature combinations. Food business operators may now freeze meat within 6 days at a maximum of 7 degrees Celsius, within 14 days at a maximum of 3 degrees Celsius, or under alternative scientifically supported conditions approved by competent authorities. The amendments do not apply to meat from even-toed farmed game mammals due to a lack of available data.

  • Replaces the requirement for freezing without undue delay for domestic ungulates with specific time and temperature criteria.
  • Allows freezing within 6 days of slaughter if stored and transported at a maximum of 7 degrees Celsius.
  • Allows freezing within 14 days of slaughter if stored and transported at a maximum of 3 degrees Celsius.
  • Permits alternative time and temperature conditions if food business operators provide scientific evidence demonstrating safety to competent authorities.
  • Excludes even-toed farmed game mammals from these new storage conditions, maintaining the requirement for freezing without undue delay.
  • Takes into account a scientific opinion adopted by EFSA on 3 December 2025 regarding the microbiological safety of ungulates meat.

What is asked: Feedback on the draft Commission Delegated Regulation amending Annex III to Regulation (EC) No 853/2004 regarding hygiene rules on storage conditions of fresh meat of domestic ungulates before freezing.

Tackling unjustified territorial supply constraints in the Single Market

Public consultation on Territorial Supply Constraints

Why it fits: Directly targets supply constraints and distribution practices imposed by large food and consumer goods manufacturers. (model judgment; similarity 0.52)

Deadline 4 Dec 2026 match, relevance 0.90 portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission has launched a public consultation on territorial supply constraints, which are limitations imposed by large manufacturers to prevent retailers from buying products in one Member State and reselling them in another. These practices fragment the Single Market, limit consumer choice, and lead to significant price differences for daily consumer goods across the EU. The initiative aims to introduce new tools to address these constraints beyond situations currently captured by competition law, as announced in the May 2025 Single Market Strategy. This consultation gathers input from stakeholders to better understand the impact and scale of these supply restrictions.

  • Territorial supply constraints hinder retailers from buying goods in one Member State to resell in another
  • Such practices fragment the Single Market and limit consumer choice
  • They contribute to significant price differences for daily consumer goods across the EU
  • The initiative follows the Single Market Strategy announced in May 2025
  • New tools are proposed to address these constraints beyond existing competition law

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide information and comments on territorial supply constraints, their impact on the Single Market, consumer choice, and retail prices, as well as views on potential tools to address them.

Alignment of the renewals of seven low-risk active substances to the renewals of substances with similar properties

COMMISSION IMPLEMENTING DECISION (EU) …/… on the establishment of a work programme for the assessment of applications for renewal of the approvals of low-risk active substances expiring in 2030 and 2031 in accordance with Regulation (EC) No 1107/2009 of the European Parliament and of the Council

Why it fits: Pesticide and biocidal active substances affect agricultural inputs into the food supply chain and food safety. (model judgment; similarity 0.48)

Deadline 16 Oct 2026 uncertain relevance, relevance 0.50 portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission proposes an implementing decision to establish a work programme that aligns the renewal application deadlines for seven low-risk active substances with existing deadlines for substances sharing similar properties and mechanisms. The substances affected include Isaria fumosorosea strain Apopka 97, COS-OGA, cerevisane, Pepino mosaic virus strain CH2 isolate 1906, ferric phosphate, Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain LAS02, and Trichoderma atroviride strain SC1. By grouping these renewals, the measure aims to improve efficiency and reduce duplication in assessments carried out by Member States and the European Food Safety Authority. Furthermore, rapporteur and co-rapporteur Member States are instructed to redirect freed resources toward delayed renewal procedures for other active substances, particularly candidates for substitution or those raising specific health and environmental concerns.

  • Establishes a work programme to group and align renewal procedures for seven low-risk active substances expiring in 2030 and 2031 with similar substances.
  • Postpones the application deadline for Isaria fumosorosea strain Apopka 97 to 18 April 2029 to align with Beauveria bassiana strain 203.
  • Postpones the application deadlines for COS-OGA, cerevisane, and Saccharomyces cerevisiae strain LAS02 to 28 February 2030 to align with laminarin.
  • Postpones the application deadline for Pepino mosaic virus strain CH2 isolate 1906 to 29 March 2029 to align with Mild Pepino Mosaic Virus isolates VC1 and VX1.
  • Postpones the application deadlines for ferric phosphate to 30 September 2030 and Trichoderma atroviride strain SC1 to 31 July 2029 to align with their respective counterparts.
  • Requires assigned rapporteur and co-rapporteur Member States to allocate freed resources to delayed active substance renewals exceeding three years, especially candidates for substitution.

What is asked: This is a draft Commission implementing decision published for feedback or information without an explicit open public consultation question set provided in the text.

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission is preparing a diversification package, including a new trade instrument and supporting measures, to address critical strategic dependencies on single supplier countries. The initiative aims to accelerate supply chain resilience and reduce economic security risks in critical sectors where non-EU countries may use dependencies as leverage. It combines trade policy measures based on Article 207 TFEU with single market measures based on Article 114 TFEU to establish a comprehensive regulatory framework. This call for evidence invites public and stakeholder feedback to inform the Commission's impact assessment and future legislative work.

  • Targets concentrated critical dependencies on dominant supplier countries for strategic sectors.
  • Proposes a new trade diversification instrument complemented by regulatory and financial support measures.
  • Combines trade policy measures under Article 207 TFEU and Single Market measures under Article 114 TFEU.
  • Seeks to address market gaps such as the lack of appropriate price signals and incentives for business de-risking.
  • Aims to protect the EU from supply chain disruptions, geopolitical pressure, and market volatility.

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide views on the prioritisation of actions to accelerate supply diversification, the legal framework and incentives needed to support business resilience, and any additional diversification measures the EU should consider.

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission has published an impact assessment and legislative review concerning Directive 2003/87/EC and Decision (EU) 2015/1814 on the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) and the Market Stability Reserve. This initiative serves as the first part of a broad post-2030 framework package aimed at driving competitiveness and cost-effective decarbonisation. It evaluates how stationary installations, aviation, and maritime sectors can deliver necessary emission reductions while ensuring carbon leakage protection and orderly market functioning. The review addresses the shrinking supply of allowances and adapts the system to align with intermediate 2040 targets and 2050 climate neutrality goals.

  • Reviews the EU ETS and the Market Stability Reserve to prepare for post-2030 climate targets.
  • Assesses pathways toward the 2050 climate neutrality goal and the intermediate 2040 target.
  • Focuses on industrial sectors, maritime transport, and aviation to ensure cost-effective decarbonisation.
  • Examines carbon leakage protection, free allocation benchmarks, and the funding transition through EU revenues.
  • Analyzes market functioning and allowance supply adjustments to prevent trading frictions and price volatility.
  • Accompanies related proposals simplifying monitoring, reporting, and verification regulations.
