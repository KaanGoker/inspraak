What the Commission proposes

The European Commission proposes a revision of EU public procurement rules through a new regulation that repeals the existing 2014 directives on public procurement, utilities, and concessions. The initiative aims to modernize and simplify rules, reduce administrative burdens, and enable preferences for European products in strategic sectors to secure supply chains and add EU value. It also amends multiple sector-specific regulations and directives containing procurement provisions to improve coherence and transparency. Furthermore, the proposal addresses high administrative burdens and legal uncertainty identified in previous evaluations regarding public buyers and businesses.

  • Revises and replaces Directives 2014/23/EU, 2014/24/EU, and 2014/25/EU with a single Public Procurement Act regulation.
  • Enables preference for European products in strategic sectors to ensure security of supply and EU added value.
  • Aims to modernize and simplify rules, specifically considering EU start-ups and innovators.
  • Amends various sector-specific regulations and directives that contain public procurement provisions.
  • Addresses administrative burdens and legal uncertainty that currently affect public buyers and businesses, including SMEs.

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission has published an impact assessment and legislative review concerning Directive 2003/87/EC and Decision (EU) 2015/1814 on the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) and the Market Stability Reserve. This initiative serves as the first part of a broad post-2030 framework package aimed at driving competitiveness and cost-effective decarbonisation. It evaluates how stationary installations, aviation, and maritime sectors can deliver necessary emission reductions while ensuring carbon leakage protection and orderly market functioning. The review addresses the shrinking supply of allowances and adapts the system to align with intermediate 2040 targets and 2050 climate neutrality goals.

  • Reviews the EU ETS and the Market Stability Reserve to prepare for post-2030 climate targets.
  • Assesses pathways toward the 2050 climate neutrality goal and the intermediate 2040 target.
  • Focuses on industrial sectors, maritime transport, and aviation to ensure cost-effective decarbonisation.
  • Examines carbon leakage protection, free allocation benchmarks, and the funding transition through EU revenues.
  • Analyzes market functioning and allowance supply adjustments to prevent trading frictions and price volatility.
  • Accompanies related proposals simplifying monitoring, reporting, and verification regulations.

Child Safety Online until 26 Nov 2026 good fit Broad regulation on digital child safety and platform accountability directly affects digital, media, and tech clients.

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission proposes the EU Kids Act regulation to establish a uniform digital environment for children across the European Union. The proposal introduces harmonized age limits, restricting autonomous account creation on social media and video sharing platforms for children under 15, with parental involvement allowed for older minors. It also sets out security-by-design requirements for online services, video sharing platforms, online games, AI companions, and app stores. Furthermore, the regulation establishes proportionate, privacy-compliant age-verification frameworks and harmonized enforcement mechanisms to prevent market fragmentation.

  • Establishes a single EU-wide digital age limit and restricts autonomous account creation on social media and video sharing platforms for children under 15.
  • Sets out security-by-design requirements for social media, video platforms, online games, AI companions, and app stores.
  • Introduces proportional and privacy-respecting age-verification criteria and safeguards.
  • Defines a structured enforcement framework relying on existing structures with expedited procedures.
  • Builds on existing EU rules such as the Digital Services Act and the Artificial Intelligence Act to prevent regulatory fragmentation in the single market.

Cloud and AI Development Act until 11 Nov 2026 good fit As a major horizontal digital initiative, this regulation directly impacts multiple corporate clients across digital economy and infrastructure.

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission proposes the Cloud and AI Development Act to establish a framework of measures aimed at strengthening Europe's cloud and artificial intelligence ecosystem. The initiative focuses on three pillars, which include research and innovation for greening infrastructure, industrial investments to triple EU data processing capacity, and actions to increase secure data processing capacities for cloud providers in the EU. Ultimately, the proposal seeks to address infrastructure gaps, reduce critical dependencies on non-European providers, and enhance the Union's digital sovereignty and competitiveness.

  • Addresses the EU gap in cloud and AI infrastructure capacity through three distinct pillars.
  • Promotes research and innovation to accelerate sustainable cloud and AI computing technologies.
  • Aims to triple the EU data processing capacity over the next 5 to 7 years via industrial investments.
  • Introduces measures to increase secure data processing capacities for high-critical use cases.
  • Establishes common criteria for sovereign cloud computing services to reduce extraterritorial dependencies.
  • Promotes open-source solutions and joint public procurement activities across the public sector.

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission has launched a public consultation to establish common Union minimum performance standards for data centres located in its territory. This initiative builds on existing EU rules on energy efficiency that introduced provisions to promote the energy efficiency and sustainability of data centres. The goal is to further improve the environmental performance and sustainability of these facilities across the European Union. Respondents are invited to provide their views on the creation of these mandatory performance standards.

  • Establishment of common Union minimum performance standards for data centres
  • Focus on energy efficiency and sustainability
  • Building upon existing EU energy efficiency rules
  • Targeting data centres located within EU territory

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide input on the establishment of minimum performance standards for data centres in the EU.
