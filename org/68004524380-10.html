Cloud and AI Development Act

Proposal for a REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL establishing a framework of measures for strengthening Europe’s cloud and AI ecosystem (Cloud and AI Development Act)

Why it fits: Strengthening European cloud and artificial intelligence infrastructure directly aligns with the mission of representing technology-inspired companies. (model judgment)

Deadline 11 Nov 2026 match portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission proposes the Cloud and AI Development Act to establish a framework of measures aimed at strengthening Europe's cloud and artificial intelligence ecosystem. The initiative focuses on three pillars, which include research and innovation for greening infrastructure, industrial investments to triple EU data processing capacity, and actions to increase secure data processing capacities for cloud providers in the EU. Ultimately, the proposal seeks to address infrastructure gaps, reduce critical dependencies on non-European providers, and enhance the Union's digital sovereignty and competitiveness.

  • Addresses the EU gap in cloud and AI infrastructure capacity through three distinct pillars.
  • Promotes research and innovation to accelerate sustainable cloud and AI computing technologies.
  • Aims to triple the EU data processing capacity over the next 5 to 7 years via industrial investments.
  • Introduces measures to increase secure data processing capacities for high-critical use cases.
  • Establishes common criteria for sovereign cloud computing services to reduce extraterritorial dependencies.
  • Promotes open-source solutions and joint public procurement activities across the public sector.

Minimum performance standards for data centres in Europe

Establishment of common Union minimum performance standards for data centres located in its territory

Why it fits: Mandatory performance standards for data centres directly target core digital infrastructure operated and developed by technology members. (model judgment)

Same initiative, also open: 26059

Deadline 14 Dec 2026 match portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission is preparing an initiative to establish common Union minimum performance standards for data centres located in its territory. Building on existing reporting and rating schemes under the Energy Efficiency Directive, the proposed regulation aims to phase out unsustainable practices and technologies in new or retrofitted facilities. The initiative seeks to address rising energy and water consumption, limited waste heat reuse, and regulatory fragmentation across Member States. An impact assessment is planned for late 2026, with a legislative proposal anticipated in the second quarter of 2027.

  • Establish common EU minimum performance standards for data centres regarding energy efficiency, water use, waste-heat reuse, and grid services.
  • Form the third step in the EU sustainability framework for data centres, following the 2024 reporting scheme and the 2026 rating scheme.
  • Prevent divergent national approaches and reduce regulatory fragmentation across Member States.
  • Improve sector competitiveness and reduce operational costs by cutting energy and water waste.
  • Support energy system integration by aligning data centre operations with power grid and heat network needs.
  • Prepare an impact assessment in Q4 2026 to support the drafting of the legislative proposal for Q2 2027.

What is asked: The consultation aims to collect in-depth evidence, information, data and feedback from stakeholders on the need for and feasibility of minimum performance standards for data centres in the EU, the most appropriate way to establish them, which indicators should have standards, and what thresholds should be set.

Revision of EU rules on public procurement

Proposal for a REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL on public contracts and concessions, repealing Directives 2014/23/EU, 2014/24/EU and 2014/25/EU, and amending Regulations (EC) No 1370/2007, (EU) 2023/1542, (EU) 2024/1157, (EU) 2024/1252, (EU) 2024/1735, (EU) 2024/1781, (EU) 2024/2847, (EU) 2024/3110 and (EU) 2025/40, and Directives 2008/98/EC, (EU) 2019/882, (EU) 2022/2381, (EU) 2023/1791 and (EU) 2024/1760 (Public Procurement Act)

Why it fits: Public procurement rules enabling preferences for European strategic sectors directly affect market access for domestic technology providers. (model judgment)

Deadline 30 Nov 2026 match portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission proposes a revision of EU public procurement rules through a new regulation that repeals the existing 2014 directives on public procurement, utilities, and concessions. The initiative aims to modernize and simplify rules, reduce administrative burdens, and enable preferences for European products in strategic sectors to secure supply chains and add EU value. It also amends multiple sector-specific regulations and directives containing procurement provisions to improve coherence and transparency. Furthermore, the proposal addresses high administrative burdens and legal uncertainty identified in previous evaluations regarding public buyers and businesses.

  • Revises and replaces Directives 2014/23/EU, 2014/24/EU, and 2014/25/EU with a single Public Procurement Act regulation.
  • Enables preference for European products in strategic sectors to ensure security of supply and EU added value.
  • Aims to modernize and simplify rules, specifically considering EU start-ups and innovators.
  • Amends various sector-specific regulations and directives that contain public procurement provisions.
  • Addresses administrative burdens and legal uncertainty that currently affect public buyers and businesses, including SMEs.
