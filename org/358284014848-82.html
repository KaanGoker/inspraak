Profile as registered

Goals
Created in 2012 through a merger of 3 European trade union federations, IndustriAll European Trade Union is a federation of independent and democratic trade unions representing manual and non-manual workers in the metal, chemical, energy, mining, textile, clothing and footwear sectors and related industries and activities. We speak for 7 million working men and women united within 200 national trade union affiliates in 39 European countries. Furthermore, we are a member of the European Trade Union Confederation (ETUC) and partner of IndustriALL Global Union. We act together, with the aim to combine and optimise strength and complementarities in order to better protect and advance the rights of the workers in our industries and sectors. To achieve this, we follow EU policies related to our members and industries closely.

"Diversify EU" instrument

Commission Proposal for a Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council establishing diversification instrument to address critical strategic dependencies that expose the Union to economic security risks

Why it fits: Addressing strategic dependencies and supply chain risks directly protects European manufacturing industries and worker employment. (model judgment; similarity 0.64)

Deadline 27 Oct 2026 match, relevance 0.80 portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission is preparing a diversification package, including a new trade instrument and supporting measures, to address critical strategic dependencies on single supplier countries. The initiative aims to accelerate supply chain resilience and reduce economic security risks in critical sectors where non-EU countries may use dependencies as leverage. It combines trade policy measures based on Article 207 TFEU with single market measures based on Article 114 TFEU to establish a comprehensive regulatory framework. This call for evidence invites public and stakeholder feedback to inform the Commission's impact assessment and future legislative work.

  • Targets concentrated critical dependencies on dominant supplier countries for strategic sectors.
  • Proposes a new trade diversification instrument complemented by regulatory and financial support measures.
  • Combines trade policy measures under Article 207 TFEU and Single Market measures under Article 114 TFEU.
  • Seeks to address market gaps such as the lack of appropriate price signals and incentives for business de-risking.
  • Aims to protect the EU from supply chain disruptions, geopolitical pressure, and market volatility.

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide views on the prioritisation of actions to accelerate supply diversification, the legal framework and incentives needed to support business resilience, and any additional diversification measures the EU should consider.

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission proposes a revision of EU public procurement rules through a new regulation that repeals the existing 2014 directives on public procurement, utilities, and concessions. The initiative aims to modernize and simplify rules, reduce administrative burdens, and enable preferences for European products in strategic sectors to secure supply chains and add EU value. It also amends multiple sector-specific regulations and directives containing procurement provisions to improve coherence and transparency. Furthermore, the proposal addresses high administrative burdens and legal uncertainty identified in previous evaluations regarding public buyers and businesses.

  • Revises and replaces Directives 2014/23/EU, 2014/24/EU, and 2014/25/EU with a single Public Procurement Act regulation.
  • Enables preference for European products in strategic sectors to ensure security of supply and EU added value.
  • Aims to modernize and simplify rules, specifically considering EU start-ups and innovators.
  • Amends various sector-specific regulations and directives that contain public procurement provisions.
  • Addresses administrative burdens and legal uncertainty that currently affect public buyers and businesses, including SMEs.

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission is conducting a review of the scope, functioning, and efficiency of Regulation (EU) 2022/1031 on the International Procurement Instrument (IPI). The review evaluates the implementation of the regulation, including investigation processes, consultations, thresholds, exceptions, and the first adopted IPI measure regarding medical devices from China. It aims to assess the instrument's effectiveness in tackling procurement barriers in non-EU countries and ensuring a level playing field for EU operators. The findings will be presented in a report to the European Parliament and the Council by August 2027.

  • Review of the scope, functioning, and efficiency of the IPI Regulation pursuant to Article 14.
  • Assessment of investigation procedures, third-country consultations, thresholds, and types of measures.
  • Evaluation of the application and implementation of the first IPI measure concerning Chinese medical devices.
  • Analysis of the efficiency of online complaint tools and rules for determining origin.
  • Execution of a 12-week online public consultation through the Have your say portal.
  • Publication of a factual summary of consultation results and an implementation report to the European Parliament and the Council.

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide views and evidence on the implementation, scope, functioning, and efficiency of the IPI Regulation in tackling non-EU procurement barriers, achieving market openness, and ensuring a level playing field, as well as the application of specific measures adopted under the Regulation.

Evaluation of the industrial and agricultural autonomous tariff suspensions and quotas scheme

Proposal for the evaluation of the Communication from the Commission concerning autonomous tariff suspensions and quotas (2011/C 363/02) and related base Council Regulations (EU) 2021/2283, 1388/2013, 2021/2278 and 1387/2013.

Why it fits: Autonomous tariff suspensions and quotas directly determine trade protection and input costs for EU industrial manufacturing sectors. (model judgment; similarity 0.54)

Deadline 8 Oct 2026 match, relevance 0.70 portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 5 Oct 2026, gemini-3.8-flash-low)

The European Commission has published a call for evidence to evaluate the autonomous tariff suspensions and quotas (ATSQ) scheme for industrial and agricultural goods. The evaluation will assess whether the scheme remains fit for purpose and continues to meet its core objectives of stimulating EU economic activity, boosting business competitiveness, and modernising production structures. It aims to prepare a revision to streamline administrative arrangements, clarify requirements for granting tariff relief, reduce the frequency of legal updates, and leverage digitalisation. The initiative covers the period since the last evaluation in 2013, with work scheduled to start in Q3-2026 and conclude in Q1-2027.

  • The evaluation covers autonomous tariff suspensions and quotas, which provide temporary tariff relief on imports of raw materials, semi-finished goods, or components unavailable in sufficient quantities in the EU.
  • In 2025, imports under the scheme represented approximately EUR 35.5 billion worth of goods and generated an estimated EUR 1.6 billion in duty savings for EU producers.
  • The evaluation will assess the scheme against five standard criteria: effectiveness, efficiency, relevance, coherence, and EU added value.
  • The findings will feed into a planned revision under the regulatory deep cleaning action plan to streamline administrative procedures and lower compliance costs.
  • Specific areas under review include requirements to grant tariff relief, the frequency of regular legal updates, and the application and assessment process.
  • The initiative is led by DG TAXUD (Unit B.5) and is scheduled to run from Q3-2026 to Q1-2027, following an external study conducted between October 2024 and April 2026.

What is asked: The Commission invites feedback on the main challenges companies face under ATSQs, whether targeted duty relief on production inputs increases EU competitiveness, the costs for businesses to apply for, object to, and monitor measures, specific simplification measures to reduce administrative burdens while preserving policy objectives, potential incentives for EU producers to diversify sourcing of inputs, and any other relevant topics.

Political context, purpose and scope of the evaluation Political context Autonomous tariff suspensions and quotas (‘ATSQs’ or ‘the scheme’) are a long-standing customs policy instrument that allows for targeted and temporary tariff relief measures for EU businesses on imports of raw materials, semi-finished products or components required in European production chains.
