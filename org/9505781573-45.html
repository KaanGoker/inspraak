Tackling unjustified territorial supply constraints in the Single Market

Public consultation on Territorial Supply Constraints

Why it fits: Territorial supply constraints directly affect consumer goods availability and retail prices for consumers across the single market. (model judgment; similarity 0.53)

Deadline 4 Dec 2026 match, relevance 1.00 portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission has launched a public consultation on territorial supply constraints, which are limitations imposed by large manufacturers to prevent retailers from buying products in one Member State and reselling them in another. These practices fragment the Single Market, limit consumer choice, and lead to significant price differences for daily consumer goods across the EU. The initiative aims to introduce new tools to address these constraints beyond situations currently captured by competition law, as announced in the May 2025 Single Market Strategy. This consultation gathers input from stakeholders to better understand the impact and scale of these supply restrictions.

  • Territorial supply constraints hinder retailers from buying goods in one Member State to resell in another
  • Such practices fragment the Single Market and limit consumer choice
  • They contribute to significant price differences for daily consumer goods across the EU
  • The initiative follows the Single Market Strategy announced in May 2025
  • New tools are proposed to address these constraints beyond existing competition law

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide information and comments on territorial supply constraints, their impact on the Single Market, consumer choice, and retail prices, as well as views on potential tools to address them.

Child Safety Online

Proposal for a REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL EU KIDS ACT - 'EU Keeping Internet Digital Spaces Accountable and Trustworthy'

Why it fits: Protecting minor users on digital platforms and social media is a core consumer protection and digital safety priority. (model judgment; similarity 0.52)

Deadline 26 Nov 2026 match, relevance 1.00 portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission proposes the EU Kids Act regulation to establish a uniform digital environment for children across the European Union. The proposal introduces harmonized age limits, restricting autonomous account creation on social media and video sharing platforms for children under 15, with parental involvement allowed for older minors. It also sets out security-by-design requirements for online services, video sharing platforms, online games, AI companions, and app stores. Furthermore, the regulation establishes proportionate, privacy-compliant age-verification frameworks and harmonized enforcement mechanisms to prevent market fragmentation.

  • Establishes a single EU-wide digital age limit and restricts autonomous account creation on social media and video sharing platforms for children under 15.
  • Sets out security-by-design requirements for social media, video platforms, online games, AI companions, and app stores.
  • Introduces proportional and privacy-respecting age-verification criteria and safeguards.
  • Defines a structured enforcement framework relying on existing structures with expedited procedures.
  • Builds on existing EU rules such as the Digital Services Act and the Artificial Intelligence Act to prevent regulatory fragmentation in the single market.

Why it fits: Cross-border jurisdiction and enforcement of civil judgments directly impact consumers seeking legal redress against foreign traders. (model judgment; similarity 0.58)

Same initiative, also open: 25820

Deadline 24 Nov 2026 match, relevance 0.70 portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission has launched an initiative to review Regulation (EU) No 1215/2012 on jurisdiction and the recognition and enforcement of judgments in civil and commercial matters. The initiative aims to simplify, clarify, and modernize the existing rules to reduce litigation costs, address legal fragmentation, and support the EU single market and competitiveness. It will be supported by a back-to-back evaluation and impact assessment, with a legislative proposal planned for the fourth quarter of 2027. The review addresses challenges identified in an application report, stakeholder consultations, and Court of Justice case law.

  • Review and potential legislative amendment of Regulation (EU) No 1215/2012.
  • Address inconsistent jurisdiction rules, particularly for defendants outside the EU.
  • Improve procedural tools for collective redress and assess aspects relevant to strategic lawsuits against public participation (SLAPs).
  • Evaluate parallel cross-border enforcement mechanisms under Regulation (EC) 805/2004.
  • Consider policy options ranging from the baseline scenario to targeted clarification, simplification, and modernization.
  • Planned adoption of the initiative in the fourth quarter of 2027.

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide views on the Commission's understanding of the problem and possible solutions, and to give any relevant information including on the possible impacts of the different options.

Minimum values for the electrochemical performance and durability of rechargeable industrial batteries

COMMISSION DELEGATED REGULATION (EU) …/... supplementing and amending Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 of the European Parliament and of the Council by laying down minimum values for the electrochemical performance and durability of rechargeable industrial batteries, technical and safety parameters, and standards concerning the battery passport

Why it fits: While covering industrial batteries, standards for residential storage touch consumer energy equipment. (model judgment; similarity 0.53)

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

This draft Commission Delegated Regulation supplements Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 by establishing minimum values for the electrochemical performance and durability of rechargeable industrial batteries with a capacity greater than 2 kWh. It sets specific thresholds for parameters such as capacity fade, expected lifetime in cycles or years, and energy round trip efficiency across different battery categories including residential storage, repetitive energy supply, and on-demand batteries. The act also outlines measurement and calculation methods, stress factor determinations, and rules for batteries that have undergone preparation for re-use, repurposing, or remanufacturing. Products designed for defence purposes are excluded from the scope of these requirements.

  • Establishes minimum performance and durability values for rechargeable industrial batteries exceeding 2 kWh capacity, excluding those with exclusively external storage or for defence purposes.
  • Sets specific criteria for residential battery energy storage systems, covering capacity fade limits, expected lifetime in cycles, and a minimum energy round trip efficiency greater than 93%.
  • Defines requirements for repetitive energy supply batteries and lead-based variants, detailing distinct capacity fade thresholds and cycle life expectations adjusted by stress factors.
  • Sets durability rules for on-demand batteries, requiring an expected lifetime in years multiplied by a stress factor to exceed 10 years.
  • Specifies measurement and calculation methods for capacity fade, expected lifetime, and round trip efficiency, incorporating stress factors related to temperature, depth of discharge, and C-rates.
  • Provides rules for batteries that have been subjected to preparation for re-use, repurposing, or remanufacturing, including compliance pathways based on previous use and operational data.
