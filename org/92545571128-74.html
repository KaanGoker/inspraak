European Transport Workers' Federation ETF

Trade unions and professional associations. Head office Bruxelles, Belgium. Register ID 92545571128-74, last updated 17 Sep 2026.

Profile as registered

Goals
The ETF was founded in 1998 to defend transport and fisheries workers' interests at European level and to make representations on their behalf to EU bodies. The ETF represents more than 230 member organisations in 41 countries, for over 5 million members. The ETF is the European branch of the International Transport Workers' Federation (ITF) and the transport branch of the ETUC. The ETF is the European social partner that represents transport and fisheries workers in seven sectoral social dialogue committees: maritime transport, civil aviation, fisheries, railways, road, ports and inland waterways.

Training and certification standards of fishers on EU vessels

Proposal for a Directive from the European Parliament and the Council on the implementation of the International Maritime Organisation Convention on Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for fishing vessel personnel onboard EU vessels

Why it fits: Implementing training and safety standards for fishing vessel personnel directly addresses the core mandate of defending fisheries workers. (model judgment; similarity 0.57)

Deadline 29 Oct 2026 match, relevance 1.00 portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission is preparing a legislative initiative to implement the International Maritime Organisation Convention on Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Fishing Vessel Personnel (STCW-F) into EU law through a directive. Currently, only ten Member States have ratified this convention, leading to significant discrepancies in training standards, safety risks, and workforce mobility across the EU. The proposed directive aims to establish harmonized minimum standards of training and certification for fishers on EU vessels, thereby improving occupational safety, environmental awareness, and the overall attractiveness of the sector. The Commission is seeking feedback from the public and stakeholders on its problem assessment, potential policy options, and their expected impacts.

  • Only ten EU Member States have ratified the IMO STCW-F Convention so far, creating uneven skill levels and standards across the Union.
  • Fishing remains one of the most dangerous occupations in Europe, with human error cited as the main cause of serious incidents.
  • The initiative seeks to establish EU-wide minimum training and certification standards, similar to existing rules for merchant seafarers.
  • Expected benefits include improved onboard safety, reduced accidents, enhanced worker mobility across Member States, and a better professional image for the sector.
  • The policy options range from maintaining the status quo to introducing a binding directive implementing the STCW-F standards.
  • Anticipated impacts involve administrative costs for setting up training modules and compliance monitoring, balanced by reduced mutual recognition costs and greater labor flexibility.

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide feedback on the Commission's understanding of the problems in the fishing sector, possible policy solutions, and any relevant information or data they may have regarding the potential impacts of the different options.

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission has published an impact assessment and legislative review concerning Directive 2003/87/EC and Decision (EU) 2015/1814 on the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) and the Market Stability Reserve. This initiative serves as the first part of a broad post-2030 framework package aimed at driving competitiveness and cost-effective decarbonisation. It evaluates how stationary installations, aviation, and maritime sectors can deliver necessary emission reductions while ensuring carbon leakage protection and orderly market functioning. The review addresses the shrinking supply of allowances and adapts the system to align with intermediate 2040 targets and 2050 climate neutrality goals.

  • Reviews the EU ETS and the Market Stability Reserve to prepare for post-2030 climate targets.
  • Assesses pathways toward the 2050 climate neutrality goal and the intermediate 2040 target.
  • Focuses on industrial sectors, maritime transport, and aviation to ensure cost-effective decarbonisation.
  • Examines carbon leakage protection, free allocation benchmarks, and the funding transition through EU revenues.
  • Analyzes market functioning and allowance supply adjustments to prevent trading frictions and price volatility.
  • Accompanies related proposals simplifying monitoring, reporting, and verification regulations.

Accreditation of aviation qualified entities for certification and oversight

COMMISSION IMPLEMENTING REGULATION (EU) …/... laying down the rules and procedures for the accreditation by the national competent authorities of qualified entities and amending certain acts in the field of aviation safety

Why it fits: Aviation safety oversight and certification entities affect operational standards and working safety for aviation personnel. (model judgment; similarity 0.52)

Deadline 21 Oct 2026 match, relevance 0.60 portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

This draft Commission Implementing Regulation establishes common rules and procedures for the accreditation of qualified entities by national competent authorities in the field of aviation safety. It sets out the requirements for establishing comprehensive accreditation systems, covering initial accreditation, continued oversight, amendment, suspension, and appeal procedures. Additionally, the regulation provides mechanisms for the transfer of accreditations between Member States and repeals overlapping provisions in several existing sector-specific Commission regulations. The measure aims to harmonize aviation safety standards and ensure effective cooperation between national authorities and the European Union Aviation Safety Agency.

  • National competent authorities must establish and maintain a comprehensive accreditation system for qualified entities.
  • The accreditation system must cover initial assessment, continued oversight, and amendment or revocation procedures.
  • Authorities can request relevant accreditation reports from the Agency or other national authorities via information repositories.
  • Rules are established for transferring an accreditation to another Member State if the original authority discontinues it on non-safety grounds.
  • Specific provisions related to qualified entities in existing regulations on aircrew, air operations, aerodromes, continuing airworthiness, air traffic controllers, and air traffic management are deleted.

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission is preparing a new EU Atlantic Maritime and Macro-regional Strategy to be adopted by June 2027, following a request from the European Council. Building on the existing Atlantic Maritime Strategy and Action Plan, the initiative aims to provide a broader, integrated territorial framework to address shared challenges and opportunities across the Atlantic region. It seeks to improve policy coordination, foster innovative solutions, strengthen multi-level governance, and increase the political weight of Atlantic priorities. The strategy targets the four EU Atlantic Member States and will be established through a non-legislative Communication without an impact assessment.

  • Development of a new EU Atlantic Maritime and Macro-regional Strategy by June 2027.
  • Builds on and integrates the Atlantic Maritime Strategy and the Atlantic Action Plan.
  • Aims to improve policy coordination across EU, national, and regional levels and funding instruments.
  • Restricted in scope to the four EU Atlantic Member States: France, Ireland, Portugal, and Spain.
  • Will incorporate a monitoring framework integrated into the Commission's biennial reports on macro-regional strategies.
  • Takes the form of a non-legislative Communication from the Commission without requiring an impact assessment.
