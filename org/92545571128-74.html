About ETF

As the organisation describes itself in the EU Transparency Register. This is what the matching reads.

Goals
The ETF was founded in 1998 to defend transport and fisheries workers' interests at European level and to make representations on their behalf to EU bodies. The ETF represents more than 230 member organisations in 41 countries, for over 5 million members. The ETF is the European branch of the International Transport Workers' Federation (ITF) and the transport branch of the ETUC. The ETF is the European social partner that represents transport and fisheries workers in seven sectoral social dialogue committees: maritime transport, civil aviation, fisheries, railways, road, ports and inland waterways.

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission has published an impact assessment and legislative review concerning Directive 2003/87/EC and Decision (EU) 2015/1814 on the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) and the Market Stability Reserve. This initiative serves as the first part of a broad post-2030 framework package aimed at driving competitiveness and cost-effective decarbonisation. It evaluates how stationary installations, aviation, and maritime sectors can deliver necessary emission reductions while ensuring carbon leakage protection and orderly market functioning. The review addresses the shrinking supply of allowances and adapts the system to align with intermediate 2040 targets and 2050 climate neutrality goals.

  • Reviews the EU ETS and the Market Stability Reserve to prepare for post-2030 climate targets.
  • Assesses pathways toward the 2050 climate neutrality goal and the intermediate 2040 target.
  • Focuses on industrial sectors, maritime transport, and aviation to ensure cost-effective decarbonisation.
  • Examines carbon leakage protection, free allocation benchmarks, and the funding transition through EU revenues.
  • Analyzes market functioning and allowance supply adjustments to prevent trading frictions and price volatility.
  • Accompanies related proposals simplifying monitoring, reporting, and verification regulations.

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission has launched a public consultation to evaluate the Balanced Approach Regulation on noise at EU airports. This regulation provides a framework for Member States to manage aviation noise and increase the sustainability of air transport. The evaluation assesses whether the current rules have successfully achieved their objectives and remain fit for purpose. It examines how the framework balances noise mitigation with the economic impact on stakeholders.

  • Evaluates the Balanced Approach Regulation governing noise at EU airports
  • Assesses whether the regulation has achieved its objective
  • Examines if the framework is still fit for purpose
  • Considers the balance between air transport sustainability and economic impacts on stakeholders

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide feedback on the performance, effectiveness, and continued relevance of the Balanced Approach Regulation.

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission is preparing a new EU Atlantic Maritime and Macro-regional Strategy to be adopted by June 2027, following a request from the European Council. Building on the existing Atlantic Maritime Strategy and Action Plan, the initiative aims to provide a broader, integrated territorial framework to address shared challenges and opportunities across the Atlantic region. It seeks to improve policy coordination, foster innovative solutions, strengthen multi-level governance, and increase the political weight of Atlantic priorities. The strategy targets the four EU Atlantic Member States and will be established through a non-legislative Communication without an impact assessment.

  • Development of a new EU Atlantic Maritime and Macro-regional Strategy by June 2027.
  • Builds on and integrates the Atlantic Maritime Strategy and the Atlantic Action Plan.
  • Aims to improve policy coordination across EU, national, and regional levels and funding instruments.
  • Restricted in scope to the four EU Atlantic Member States: France, Ireland, Portugal, and Spain.
  • Will incorporate a monitoring framework integrated into the Commission's biennial reports on macro-regional strategies.
  • Takes the form of a non-legislative Communication from the Commission without requiring an impact assessment.

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide views on the Commission's understanding of the problem and possible solutions, and to share any relevant information they may have regarding the Atlantic maritime and macro-regional strategy initiative.

Revision of EU rules on public procurement until 30 Nov 2026 possible fit Public procurement revisions amend public transport service rules and influence labor criteria for contracted transport workers.

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission proposes a revision of EU public procurement rules through a new regulation that repeals the existing 2014 directives on public procurement, utilities, and concessions. The initiative aims to modernize and simplify rules, reduce administrative burdens, and enable preferences for European products in strategic sectors to secure supply chains and add EU value. It also amends multiple sector-specific regulations and directives containing procurement provisions to improve coherence and transparency. Furthermore, the proposal addresses high administrative burdens and legal uncertainty identified in previous evaluations regarding public buyers and businesses.

  • Revises and replaces Directives 2014/23/EU, 2014/24/EU, and 2014/25/EU with a single Public Procurement Act regulation.
  • Enables preference for European products in strategic sectors to ensure security of supply and EU added value.
  • Aims to modernize and simplify rules, specifically considering EU start-ups and innovators.
  • Amends various sector-specific regulations and directives that contain public procurement provisions.
  • Addresses administrative burdens and legal uncertainty that currently affect public buyers and businesses, including SMEs.
