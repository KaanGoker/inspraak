Cloud and AI Development Act until 11 Nov 2026 good fit A major horizontal framework on cloud and artificial intelligence infrastructure directly impacts the digital economy for Belgian businesses.

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission proposes the Cloud and AI Development Act to establish a framework of measures aimed at strengthening Europe's cloud and artificial intelligence ecosystem. The initiative focuses on three pillars, which include research and innovation for greening infrastructure, industrial investments to triple EU data processing capacity, and actions to increase secure data processing capacities for cloud providers in the EU. Ultimately, the proposal seeks to address infrastructure gaps, reduce critical dependencies on non-European providers, and enhance the Union's digital sovereignty and competitiveness.

  • Addresses the EU gap in cloud and AI infrastructure capacity through three distinct pillars.
  • Promotes research and innovation to accelerate sustainable cloud and AI computing technologies.
  • Aims to triple the EU data processing capacity over the next 5 to 7 years via industrial investments.
  • Introduces measures to increase secure data processing capacities for high-critical use cases.
  • Establishes common criteria for sovereign cloud computing services to reduce extraterritorial dependencies.
  • Promotes open-source solutions and joint public procurement activities across the public sector.

Tackling unjustified territorial supply constraints in the Single Market until 4 Dec 2026 good fit Single market barriers and cross border supply restrictions directly affect member companies across industry and commerce.

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission has launched a public consultation on territorial supply constraints, which are limitations imposed by large manufacturers to prevent retailers from buying products in one Member State and reselling them in another. These practices fragment the Single Market, limit consumer choice, and lead to significant price differences for daily consumer goods across the EU. The initiative aims to introduce new tools to address these constraints beyond situations currently captured by competition law, as announced in the May 2025 Single Market Strategy. This consultation gathers input from stakeholders to better understand the impact and scale of these supply restrictions.

  • Territorial supply constraints hinder retailers from buying goods in one Member State to resell in another
  • Such practices fragment the Single Market and limit consumer choice
  • They contribute to significant price differences for daily consumer goods across the EU
  • The initiative follows the Single Market Strategy announced in May 2025
  • New tools are proposed to address these constraints beyond existing competition law

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide information and comments on territorial supply constraints, their impact on the Single Market, consumer choice, and retail prices, as well as views on potential tools to address them.

Review of the International Procurement Instrument until 1 Dec 2026 good fit Reciprocal access to foreign public procurement markets is an important international trade concern for Belgian enterprises.

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission is conducting a review of the scope, functioning, and efficiency of Regulation (EU) 2022/1031 on the International Procurement Instrument (IPI). The review evaluates the implementation of the regulation, including investigation processes, consultations, thresholds, exceptions, and the first adopted IPI measure regarding medical devices from China. It aims to assess the instrument's effectiveness in tackling procurement barriers in non-EU countries and ensuring a level playing field for EU operators. The findings will be presented in a report to the European Parliament and the Council by August 2027.

  • Review of the scope, functioning, and efficiency of the IPI Regulation pursuant to Article 14.
  • Assessment of investigation procedures, third-country consultations, thresholds, and types of measures.
  • Evaluation of the application and implementation of the first IPI measure concerning Chinese medical devices.
  • Analysis of the efficiency of online complaint tools and rules for determining origin.
  • Execution of a 12-week online public consultation through the Have your say portal.
  • Publication of a factual summary of consultation results and an implementation report to the European Parliament and the Council.

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide views and evidence on the implementation, scope, functioning, and efficiency of the IPI Regulation in tackling non-EU procurement barriers, achieving market openness, and ensuring a level playing field, as well as the application of specific measures adopted under the Regulation.

Minimum performance standards for data centres in Europe until 14 Dec 2026 possible fit Specific technical energy performance rules for data centre facilities are narrow and peripheral for a general cross sector federation.

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission is preparing an initiative to establish common Union minimum performance standards for data centres located in its territory. Building on existing reporting and rating schemes under the Energy Efficiency Directive, the proposed regulation aims to phase out unsustainable practices and technologies in new or retrofitted facilities. The initiative seeks to address rising energy and water consumption, limited waste heat reuse, and regulatory fragmentation across Member States. An impact assessment is planned for late 2026, with a legislative proposal anticipated in the second quarter of 2027.

  • Establish common EU minimum performance standards for data centres regarding energy efficiency, water use, waste-heat reuse, and grid services.
  • Form the third step in the EU sustainability framework for data centres, following the 2024 reporting scheme and the 2026 rating scheme.
  • Prevent divergent national approaches and reduce regulatory fragmentation across Member States.
  • Improve sector competitiveness and reduce operational costs by cutting energy and water waste.
  • Support energy system integration by aligning data centre operations with power grid and heat network needs.
  • Prepare an impact assessment in Q4 2026 to support the drafting of the legislative proposal for Q2 2027.

What is asked: The consultation aims to collect in-depth evidence, information, data and feedback from stakeholders on the need for and feasibility of minimum performance standards for data centres in the EU, the most appropriate way to establish them, which indicators should have standards, and what thresholds should be set.
