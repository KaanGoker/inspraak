Profile as registered

Goals
Set up in 1974, the EEB is Europe’s largest and most inclusive network of environmental citizens’ groups - and the only one that works on such a broad range of issues. It currently consists of over 190 member organisations in 41 countries, including a growing number of networks, and representing some 30 million individual members and supporters. Our vision is of a better future where people and nature thrive together. The next generation deserves a healthy planet. We believe in a world where equal, just, peaceful and democratic societies can prosper. A world with rich biodiversity and a safe climate. A world where laws and policies promote health and wellbeing while respecting nature. We believe that Europe has a crucial role to play in building this future and aim to contribute to efforts related to creating a better environment in the European Union and beyond.

EU emissions trading system for maritime, aviation and stationary installations, and market stability reserve - review

Proposal for a DIRECTIVE OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL amending Directive 2003/87/EC and Decision (EU) 2015/1814 as regards driving competitiveness and cost-effective decarbonisation

Why it fits: Revising the EU emissions trading system for decarbonisation is a core climate priority for environmental groups. (model judgment)

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission has published an impact assessment and legislative review concerning Directive 2003/87/EC and Decision (EU) 2015/1814 on the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) and the Market Stability Reserve. This initiative serves as the first part of a broad post-2030 framework package aimed at driving competitiveness and cost-effective decarbonisation. It evaluates how stationary installations, aviation, and maritime sectors can deliver necessary emission reductions while ensuring carbon leakage protection and orderly market functioning. The review addresses the shrinking supply of allowances and adapts the system to align with intermediate 2040 targets and 2050 climate neutrality goals.

  • Reviews the EU ETS and the Market Stability Reserve to prepare for post-2030 climate targets.
  • Assesses pathways toward the 2050 climate neutrality goal and the intermediate 2040 target.
  • Focuses on industrial sectors, maritime transport, and aviation to ensure cost-effective decarbonisation.
  • Examines carbon leakage protection, free allocation benchmarks, and the funding transition through EU revenues.
  • Analyzes market functioning and allowance supply adjustments to prevent trading frictions and price volatility.
  • Accompanies related proposals simplifying monitoring, reporting, and verification regulations.

Why it fits: Tracking packaging and municipal waste reduction targets is central to circular economy and environmental advocacy. (model judgment)

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission is preparing the 2027 early warning report to assess Member States progress towards 2030 targets for municipal waste recycling, packaging waste reduction and recycling, and food waste reduction, alongside the 2035 municipal waste landfilling reduction target. The report identifies countries at risk of missing these targets and provides tailored recommendations and best practices to improve their performance. This initiative fulfils a legal obligation under EU waste directives without introducing new policy objectives or requiring an impact assessment. It focuses on evaluating national policies and implementation measures that directly impact waste prevention, separate collection, sorting, and recycling.

  • Assess progress towards 2030 waste reduction and recycling targets and 2035 landfill reduction targets.
  • Identify Member States at risk of not meeting the established targets.
  • Provide general and country-specific recommendations alongside best practice sharing.
  • Fulfill legal reporting obligations under the Waste Framework Directive, Packaging and Packaging Waste Directive, and Landfill Directive.
  • Incorporate data and methodology developed by the European Environment Agency and its European Topic Centre.
  • Include dedicated country-specific annexes for Member States identified at risk.

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide views on the Commission's understanding of the problem and possible solutions, identify implementation challenges present in Member States, suggest measures to improve waste management performance, and provide input on what general and country-specific recommendations should be made.

COMMISSION IMPLEMENTING DECISION (EU) …/… laying down rules for the application of Directive (EU) 2016/2284 of the European Parliament and of the Council as regards the common format for national air pollution control programmes and repealing Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2018/1522

Why it fits: National air pollution control programmes are directly tied to air quality and environmental health protection. (model judgment)

Deadline 21 Oct 2026 match portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

This Commission implementing decision lays down a revised common format for national air pollution control programmes under Directive (EU) 2016/2284. It aims to simplify and clarify reporting requirements, improve data comparability, and support Member States in planning measures to meet national emission reduction commitments. The decision repeals the previous Implementing Decision (EU) 2018/1522 and introduces a 12-month transitional period allowing Member States to use either the old or the new format. The annex details specific reporting sections covering general information, policy frameworks, emission inventories, air quality status, and projected impacts.

  • Establishes a revised common format for initial and updated National Air Pollution Control Programmes (NAPCPs).
  • Repeals Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2018/1522.
  • Grants a 12-month transitional period where Member States can use either the previous or the new format.
  • Requires structured reporting on general administrative information, air quality policy frameworks, current emission and air quality situations, transboundary impacts, and adopted policies and measures.
  • Mandates quantitative projections of future emissions for key pollutants including SO2, NOx, NMVOC, NH3, and PM2.5.

Minimum performance standards for data centres in Europe

Establishment of common Union minimum performance standards for data centres located in its territory

Why it fits: Limiting energy and water consumption of data centres directly aligns with resource efficiency and climate goals. (model judgment)

Same initiative, also open: 26059

Deadline 14 Dec 2026 match portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission is preparing an initiative to establish common Union minimum performance standards for data centres located in its territory. Building on existing reporting and rating schemes under the Energy Efficiency Directive, the proposed regulation aims to phase out unsustainable practices and technologies in new or retrofitted facilities. The initiative seeks to address rising energy and water consumption, limited waste heat reuse, and regulatory fragmentation across Member States. An impact assessment is planned for late 2026, with a legislative proposal anticipated in the second quarter of 2027.

  • Establish common EU minimum performance standards for data centres regarding energy efficiency, water use, waste-heat reuse, and grid services.
  • Form the third step in the EU sustainability framework for data centres, following the 2024 reporting scheme and the 2026 rating scheme.
  • Prevent divergent national approaches and reduce regulatory fragmentation across Member States.
  • Improve sector competitiveness and reduce operational costs by cutting energy and water waste.
  • Support energy system integration by aligning data centre operations with power grid and heat network needs.
  • Prepare an impact assessment in Q4 2026 to support the drafting of the legislative proposal for Q2 2027.

What is asked: The consultation aims to collect in-depth evidence, information, data and feedback from stakeholders on the need for and feasibility of minimum performance standards for data centres in the EU, the most appropriate way to establish them, which indicators should have standards, and what thresholds should be set.
