Revision of Schengen short-stay visa procedures (recast)

Proposal for a Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council establishing a Union Code on Visas (recast)

Why it fits: Schengen visa procedures directly affect third-country nationals, readmission policies, and access to European territory. (model judgment)

Deadline 6 Oct 2026 match portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission proposes a legislative recast of the EU Visa Code to make Schengen short-stay visa rules more strategic, secure, and effective. The initiative aims to improve cooperation with third countries on return, readmission, and security, while providing a framework to respond to unforeseen events or hostile actions. It also seeks to facilitate legitimate travel, particularly for business travellers linked to trusted companies, and addresses technical aspects related to the digitalisation of visa procedures. Additionally, the proposal explores the feasibility of an additional charge on top of the Schengen visa fee to support the common visa policy.

  • Strengthens tools linking visa policy to third-country cooperation on return, readmission, and border management.
  • Establishes a mechanism to suspend or restrict visa applications from specific travellers in response to hostile actions or emergencies.
  • Introduces a common EU approach to identify trusted companies and facilitate travel for their business representatives.
  • Improves local consular adaptations and addresses technical inconsistencies related to the digitalisation of visas.
  • Explores the introduction of a harmonised additional charge levied at EU level on top of the Schengen visa fee.
  • Includes consequential amendments to the Visa Regulation and the European Border and Coast Guard Regulation.

What is asked: Views on the Commission's understanding of the problem and possible solutions, along with any relevant information respondents may have.

Political context, problem definition and subsidiarity check Political context In January 2026, the European Commission adopted the first EU visa policy strategy setting out a comprehensive framework to make the EU's visa rules more secure, more efficient, and better aligned with Europe's strategic interests. The strategy identifies the revision (recast) of the EU's common rules on short-stay visas (Regulation (EC) No 810/2009 - the Visa Code) as a key priority action. The initiative follows from the strategy's call to modernise the tools available to the EU to respond to challenges such as insufficient cooperation from third countries (non-EU countries) on the return of irregular migrants and hostile actions by foreign states, as well as the need to better facilitate legitimate travel for tourists, business travellers and people coming to the EU to use their talents.

Action at EU level will also allow common responses to challenges linked to security, migration, border management and return and readmission, while ensuring that any facilitation for legitimate travellers is based on strong common safeguards. Basis for EU action (legal basis and subsidiarity check) Legal basis The legal basis for the initiative is point (a) of Article 77(2) of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU), which empowers the EU to develop measures concerning ‘the common policy on visas and other short- stay residence permits’. This falls within the area of freedom, security and justice, where competence is shared between the European Union and the Member States in accordance with Article 4(2)(j) TFEU. Practical need for EU action The objectives of this initiative cannot be sufficiently achieved by Member States acting alone. Regulation (EC) No 810/2009 is an EU act and can only be amended at EU level. Short-stay visas issued by one Member State normally allow travel throughout the Schengen area for up to 90 days in any 180-day period.

There is potential to make the current mechanism more strategic and to use it more effectively, for example by exploring a more flexible choice of restrictive visa measures and a swifter and more focused response to a serious deterioration in cooperation, while ensuring that any EU measures remain proportionate to the situation addressed.

EU Strategy on victims’ rights (2026 - 2030)

Strategy on Victims' Rights - Public Consultation

Why it fits: Victims rights frameworks plausibly protect vulnerable migrants and refugees who fall victim to crimes. (model judgment)

Deadline 5 Nov 2026 uncertain relevance portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission has launched a public consultation for the EU Strategy on victims’ rights covering the period from 2026 to 2030. This initiative aims to consolidate the EU framework for protecting victims of crime and ensures that these rights are effectively exercised across all Member States. It builds upon the achievements of the previous 2020 to 2025 strategy while addressing newly emerging threats. Furthermore, the strategy will complement the provisionally agreed revision of the Victims’ rights Directive with non-legislative measures.

  • Consolidates the EU framework for protecting victims of crime
  • Focuses on the effective practical exercise of victims' rights across Member States
  • Builds on the achievements of the 2020 to 2025 victims' rights strategy
  • Addresses new emerging threats
  • Complements the provisionally agreed revision of the Victims’ rights Directive with non-legislative measures

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide input on the EU strategy on victims' rights for the period 2026 to 2030, including views on protecting victims, addressing emerging threats, and ensuring effective practical application across Member States.

Why it fits: Europol operations and police cooperation tangentially intersect with border control and migrant rights. (model judgment)

Deadline 6 Nov 2026 uncertain relevance portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission has proposed a new regulation on the European Union Agency for Law Enforcement Cooperation (Europol) to reinforce its operational role in combating serious cross-border crime and terrorism. The initiative replaces Regulation (EU) 2016/794, updates Europol's mandate to address a changing security landscape, and provides for increased resources and staff. It also establishes structured cooperation between Europol and eu-LISA for information management and ICT services while ensuring full alignment with EU data protection frameworks.

  • Replaces Regulation (EU) 2016/794 to establish a revised mandate for Europol.
  • Aims to enhance Europol's operational capabilities against online and offline serious crime and terrorism.
  • Establishes structured cooperation between Europol and eu-LISA for information systems and communication infrastructure.
  • Allocates an estimated EUR 1.053 billion and 910 additional staff posts over the 2028-2034 MFF.
  • Maintains alignment with the EU data protection framework and fundamental rights standards.

Eurojust Regulation Revision

Proposal for a REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL on the European Union Agency for Criminal Justice Cooperation (Eurojust) and repealing Regulation (EU) 2018/1727

Why it fits: Eurojust criminal justice cooperation is peripheral to the legal rights of asylum seekers. (model judgment)

Deadline 4 Nov 2026 uncertain relevance portal

What the Commission proposes (summary from the document, cached 3 Oct 2026, gemini-3.5-flash-lite)

The European Commission proposes a new regulation on the European Union Agency for Criminal Justice Cooperation (Eurojust) to repeal and replace Regulation (EU) 2018/1727. This initiative responds to an evaluation showing that while Eurojust is indispensable, its operations need greater efficiency and a wider mandate to tackle modern cross-border crime. The proposal expands Eurojust's competence into emerging areas such as cybercrime, gender-based violence, and violations of EU restrictive measures, while transforming it into a more proactive analytical hub. It also aims to streamline the agency's internal structure and improve cooperation with partners like Europol and the European Public Prosecutor Office.

  • Repeals and replaces Regulation (EU) 2018/1727 to establish a renewed legal framework for Eurojust.
  • Expands Eurojust's mandate to cover emerging crime areas including cybercrime, gender-based violence, and violations of EU restrictive measures.
  • Transforms Eurojust from a reactive coordination body into a strategic and analytical hub for cross-border judicial cooperation.
  • Standardises National Members' status and powers to improve governance and operational efficiency.
  • Addresses findings from the 2025 evaluation regarding complex decision-making, wide case scope, and slow procedures with third countries.
  • Aligns with related revisions of the Europol Regulation, the European Investigation Order Directive, and the European Union Data Protection Regulation.

What is asked: The Commission sought the views of citizens, Member States, and other relevant stakeholders on Eurojust and the Eurojust Regulation via an online public consultation to inform the impact assessment for the revision of the Eurojust Regulation.
