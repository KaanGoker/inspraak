Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission has launched a public consultation for the EU Strategy on victims’ rights covering the period from 2026 to 2030. This initiative aims to consolidate the EU framework for protecting victims of crime and ensures that these rights are effectively exercised across all Member States. It builds upon the achievements of the previous 2020 to 2025 strategy while addressing newly emerging threats. Furthermore, the strategy will complement the provisionally agreed revision of the Victims’ rights Directive with non-legislative measures.

  • Consolidates the EU framework for protecting victims of crime
  • Focuses on the effective practical exercise of victims' rights across Member States
  • Builds on the achievements of the 2020 to 2025 victims' rights strategy
  • Addresses new emerging threats
  • Complements the provisionally agreed revision of the Victims’ rights Directive with non-legislative measures

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide input on the EU strategy on victims' rights for the period 2026 to 2030, including views on protecting victims, addressing emerging threats, and ensuring effective practical application across Member States.

EU emissions trading system for maritime, aviation and stationary installations, and market stability reserve - review until 3 Nov 2026 possible fit

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission has published an impact assessment and legislative review concerning Directive 2003/87/EC and Decision (EU) 2015/1814 on the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) and the Market Stability Reserve. This initiative serves as the first part of a broad post-2030 framework package aimed at driving competitiveness and cost-effective decarbonisation. It evaluates how stationary installations, aviation, and maritime sectors can deliver necessary emission reductions while ensuring carbon leakage protection and orderly market functioning. The review addresses the shrinking supply of allowances and adapts the system to align with intermediate 2040 targets and 2050 climate neutrality goals.

  • Reviews the EU ETS and the Market Stability Reserve to prepare for post-2030 climate targets.
  • Assesses pathways toward the 2050 climate neutrality goal and the intermediate 2040 target.
  • Focuses on industrial sectors, maritime transport, and aviation to ensure cost-effective decarbonisation.
  • Examines carbon leakage protection, free allocation benchmarks, and the funding transition through EU revenues.
  • Analyzes market functioning and allowance supply adjustments to prevent trading frictions and price volatility.
  • Accompanies related proposals simplifying monitoring, reporting, and verification regulations.

Omnibus on taxation until 5 Nov 2026 possible fit

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The proposal introduces a Council Directive, known as the Omnibus on Taxation, which amends six existing directives in the field of direct taxation to simplify the EU corporate tax framework and support business growth and competitiveness. It aims to reduce administrative and compliance burdens for cross-border activities while maintaining high standards of protection against tax avoidance and abusive practices. The initiative aligns existing rules with international tax developments, such as the Pillar Two global minimum tax, and addresses procedural and legal obstacles in areas like withholding taxes and corporate reorganisations. Overall, the measures seek to improve legal certainty and ensure the coherent functioning of the internal market without lowering tax standards.

  • Amends Directives 2003/49/EC, 2009/133/EC, 2011/96/EU, (EU) 2016/1164, (EU) 2017/1852, and (EU) 2025/50.
  • Simplifies the Union framework on direct taxation to reduce compliance costs for businesses.
  • Eliminates overlapping provisions and addresses practical issues arising from the interaction with the Pillar Two Directive.
  • Removes procedural barriers and legal uncertainty linked to withholding tax relief.
  • Maintains existing safeguards and high levels of protection against tax avoidance and evasion.

What is asked: Respondents expressed their views on simplifying existing EU tax rules to improve the functioning of the internal market and enhance Europe's attractiveness for investment and business.

Full consultation page Answer on the Commission's site

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission has proposed a new regulation on the European Union Agency for Law Enforcement Cooperation (Europol) to reinforce its operational role in combating serious cross-border crime and terrorism. The initiative replaces Regulation (EU) 2016/794, updates Europol's mandate to address a changing security landscape, and provides for increased resources and staff. It also establishes structured cooperation between Europol and eu-LISA for information management and ICT services while ensuring full alignment with EU data protection frameworks.

  • Replaces Regulation (EU) 2016/794 to establish a revised mandate for Europol.
  • Aims to enhance Europol's operational capabilities against online and offline serious crime and terrorism.
  • Establishes structured cooperation between Europol and eu-LISA for information systems and communication infrastructure.
  • Allocates an estimated EUR 1.053 billion and 910 additional staff posts over the 2028-2034 MFF.
  • Maintains alignment with the EU data protection framework and fundamental rights standards.

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission proposes a new regulation on the European Union Agency for Criminal Justice Cooperation (Eurojust) to repeal and replace Regulation (EU) 2018/1727. This initiative responds to an evaluation showing that while Eurojust is indispensable, its operations need greater efficiency and a wider mandate to tackle modern cross-border crime. The proposal expands Eurojust's competence into emerging areas such as cybercrime, gender-based violence, and violations of EU restrictive measures, while transforming it into a more proactive analytical hub. It also aims to streamline the agency's internal structure and improve cooperation with partners like Europol and the European Public Prosecutor Office.

  • Repeals and replaces Regulation (EU) 2018/1727 to establish a renewed legal framework for Eurojust.
  • Expands Eurojust's mandate to cover emerging crime areas including cybercrime, gender-based violence, and violations of EU restrictive measures.
  • Transforms Eurojust from a reactive coordination body into a strategic and analytical hub for cross-border judicial cooperation.
  • Standardises National Members' status and powers to improve governance and operational efficiency.
  • Addresses findings from the 2025 evaluation regarding complex decision-making, wide case scope, and slow procedures with third countries.
  • Aligns with related revisions of the Europol Regulation, the European Investigation Order Directive, and the European Union Data Protection Regulation.

What is asked: The Commission sought the views of citizens, Member States, and other relevant stakeholders on Eurojust and the Eurojust Regulation via an online public consultation to inform the impact assessment for the revision of the Eurojust Regulation.
