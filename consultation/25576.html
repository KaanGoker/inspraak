Evaluation of the Employers Sanctions Directive

Evaluation of Directive 2009/52/EC of the European Parliament and the Council of 18 June 2009 providing for minimum standards on sanctions and measures against employers of illegally staying third-country nationals

closed cfe evl fc, Ares(2026)6383112, HOME. Feedback period 27 Jul 2026 to 18 Sep 2026. Initiative on the portal. Topics: Migration and asylum.

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission will launch an evaluation of the Employers Sanctions Directive (Directive 2009/52/EC) between Q2 2026 and Q1 2027 to assess whether it remains fit for purpose, effective, efficient, relevant, coherent, and provides EU added value. The evaluation will assess the application and impact of provisions concerning prevention, inspections, administrative and criminal sanctions, and protective safeguards for illegally staying third-country workers across 25 Member States for the period 2012-2025. It will also examine cross-border cooperation, document fraud, effects on labour exploitation, and the administrative burden placed on Member States and employers. The findings will provide the basis for the Commission to determine whether to propose a revision of the Directive and carry out a subsequent impact assessment, or whether no revision is necessary.

  • The evaluation covers the implementation and enforcement of Directive 2009/52/EC from 2012 to 2025 across the 25 participating Member States (excluding Denmark and Ireland).
  • The assessment will focus on provisions relating to inspections, administrative and criminal sanctions, subcontracting liability, prevention measures, and protective safeguards such as complaints mechanisms and back payments.
  • The initiative addresses shortcomings identified in the May 2026 implementation report, notably uneven inspection coverage, varying application of sanctions, and uncertain protection of workers' rights.
  • The evaluation will analyse coherence with the European Labour Authority's mandate, the Seasonal Workers Directive implementation, and EU actions countering human trafficking for labour exploitation.
  • It will evaluate administrative burden on Member States and employers, exploring potential scope for simplification.
  • Findings will determine whether the Commission decides to revise the Directive with an accompanying impact assessment or maintain the existing framework.

Affected, as named in the text: Employers; SMEs; Third-country nationals staying in the EU illegally; Member States; Enforcement authorities; Labour inspectorates; Social partners; Representative SME organisations and business associations; Scientific researchers, academic organisations, learned societies and scientific associations; EU agencies and expert groups.

What is asked: The Commission invites feedback to collect evidence on the implementation, effectiveness, efficiency, relevance, coherence, and EU added value of the Directive, including practical experiences regarding enforcement, sanctions, inspections, and administrative burdens. Representative SME organisations and business associations are asked to provide views on the impact on SMEs. In addition, scientific researchers, academic organisations, learned societies, and scientific associations are invited to submit published and pre-print research, data, analyses, and syntheses of the current state of knowledge in labour migration, labour market regulation, and labour exploitation.

Dossier summary from the portal

The Commission will launch an evaluation of the Directive in 2026 to determine whether it remains fit for purpose and what lessons can be learned to improve its effectiveness. The evaluation will also analyse the efficiency and continuing relevance of the Directive and will stress-test it to ensure that it remains relevant and fit for purpose. Based on the evaluation, the Commission will consider whether to amend or clarify the current legal framework.
