EU emissions trading system for maritime, aviation and stationary installations, and market stability reserve - review

Full official titleProposal for a DIRECTIVE OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL amending Directive 2003/87/EC and Decision (EU) 2015/1814 as regards driving competitiveness and cost-effective decarbonisation

open on 5 Oct 2026 Open for answers from 25 Aug 2026 to 3 Nov 2026 (open). Answer on the Commission's site. Topics: Climate action.

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission has published an impact assessment and legislative review concerning Directive 2003/87/EC and Decision (EU) 2015/1814 on the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) and the Market Stability Reserve. This initiative serves as the first part of a broad post-2030 framework package aimed at driving competitiveness and cost-effective decarbonisation. It evaluates how stationary installations, aviation, and maritime sectors can deliver necessary emission reductions while ensuring carbon leakage protection and orderly market functioning. The review addresses the shrinking supply of allowances and adapts the system to align with intermediate 2040 targets and 2050 climate neutrality goals.

  • Reviews the EU ETS and the Market Stability Reserve to prepare for post-2030 climate targets.
  • Assesses pathways toward the 2050 climate neutrality goal and the intermediate 2040 target.
  • Focuses on industrial sectors, maritime transport, and aviation to ensure cost-effective decarbonisation.
  • Examines carbon leakage protection, free allocation benchmarks, and the funding transition through EU revenues.
  • Analyzes market functioning and allowance supply adjustments to prevent trading frictions and price volatility.
  • Accompanies related proposals simplifying monitoring, reporting, and verification regulations.

Affected, as named in the text: industrial sectors; maritime transport; aviation; stationary installations; SMEs; public authorities.

Dossier summary from the portal

The EU emissions trading system is a key policy tool to achieve the EU’s climate targets. Certain elements of the system are subject to review by 2026, including on assessing additional policies to reach those targets. In addition, the market stability reserve is also subject to review by 2026. Both are being evaluated to feed into these reviews.
