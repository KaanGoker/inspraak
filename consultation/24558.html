Guidelines on CSDDD

Full official titleOpen Public Consultation on the Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive Guidelines

closed Open for answers from 12 Jun 2026 to 14 Aug 2026. Answer on the Commission's site. Topics: Justice and fundamental rights.

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission is developing guidelines to support the implementation of Directive 2024/1760 on corporate sustainability due diligence (CSDDD). The CSDDD applies to very large EU companies and non-EU companies with significant business in the EU. Under the directive, in-scope companies must identify, prevent, and bring to an end adverse impacts on human rights and the environment across their own operations, those of their subsidiaries, and within their value chains. This open public consultation gathers feedback to inform the drafting of these implementation guidelines.

  • Development of Commission guidelines to support the implementation of Directive 2024/1760 (CSDDD).
  • Covers very large EU companies and non-EU companies with significant EU business.
  • Companies must identify actual and potential adverse impacts on human rights and the environment.
  • Companies must prevent and bring to an end identified adverse impacts.
  • Obligations apply to companies' own operations, subsidiary operations, and value chains.

Affected, as named in the text: very large EU companies; non-EU companies with significant business in the EU; subsidiaries of in-scope companies.

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide feedback on the upcoming guidelines to support the implementation of Directive 2024/1760 on corporate sustainability due diligence (CSDDD).

Dossier summary from the portal

The European Commission is developing guidelines to support the implementation of Directive 2024/1760 on corporate sustainability due diligence (CSDDD). The CSDDD targets very large EU companies, as well as non-EU companies with significant business in the EU. It aims to ensure that companies within its scope identify, prevent and bring to an end actual and potential adverse impacts on human rights and the environment in their own operations, those of their subsidiaries and in their value chains.
