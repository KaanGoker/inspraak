Ecodesign requirements for iron and steel products

Full official titleCommission Delegated Regulation establishing ecodesign requirements for sustainable and circular 'iron and steel' products under Regulation (EU) 2024/1781 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 13 June 2024 establishing a framework for the setting of ecodesign requirements for sustainable products

closed Open for answers from 20 May 2026 to 12 Aug 2026. Answer on the Commission's site. Topics: Single market.

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission has published a call for evidence for an impact assessment on a delegated regulation setting ecodesign requirements for iron and steel products under Regulation (EU) 2024/1781. The initiative aims to improve the environmental sustainability and circularity of intermediate iron and steel products while preventing internal market fragmentation. To address supply chain environmental impacts and improve circularity, the Commission considers introducing carbon footprint and recycled content information requirements, setting performance classes and minimum thresholds, and establishing digital product passport rules. Additionally, the initiative intends to define low-carbon steel to support the implementation of the proposed Industrial Accelerator Act in green public procurement and public support schemes.

  • Preparation of a delegated regulation under the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR) with an indicative timetable of Q4 2026.
  • Introduction of information requirements concerning carbon footprint, recycled content, and substances that hinder recyclability.
  • Establishment of performance classes and minimum environmental performance thresholds for iron and steel products.
  • Setting of digital product passport requirements covering all actors along the supply chain.
  • Definition of low-carbon steel to support lead markets and public procurement measures under the proposed Industrial Accelerator Act.
  • Establishment of harmonised EU-level rules under Article 114 TFEU to prevent internal market fragmentation from diverging national initiatives.

Affected, as named in the text: economic operators inside and outside the EU; manufacturers; actors involved in the supply chain; purchasers; end-users; automotive sector; construction sector; defence sector.

What is asked: Stakeholders and the public are invited to provide feedback on the Commission's understanding of the problem and possible solutions, as well as any relevant information they have, including on possible impacts of the different options.

Dossier summary from the portal

Making the manufacturing and use of steel more sustainable is a central goal of the EU Clean Industrial Deal and the Steel and Metal Action Plan. The proposed Industrial Accelerator Act introduces low-carbon requirements for steel in public procurement and public support schemes. This initiative will define low-carbon steel and set ecodesign requirements to promote the sustainable and circular design of iron and steel products and improve access to information relevant to their sustainability and circular production.
