EU Teachers Agenda: Enhancing the attractiveness of the education and training profession

Full official titleCommunication from the Commission to the European Parliament, the Council, the European Economic and Social Committee and the Committee of the Regions: Quality Education for All - A 2040 Roadmap for Strengthening Basic Skills, Elevating Teaching Careers, and Future-Proofing Schools for an age of AI

closed Open for answers from 30 Jul 2026 to 8 Sep 2026. Answer on the Commission's site. Topics: Education and training.

Grouped call: this publication belongs to 3 initiatives: EU Teachers Agenda: Enhancing the attractiveness of the education and training profession; Quality Education for All: A 2040 Roadmap; Strengthening basic skills and improving performance in school education.

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission plans to adopt an Education Package in Q4 2026 to tackle falling basic skills, growing teacher shortages, and the reliance on non-EU digital infrastructure. The initiative is set to include a Communication alongside two proposals for Council Recommendations focusing on basic skills and attractive teaching careers (the EU Teachers Agenda). It will also be accompanied by a Commission Decision to establish a European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) for education. EU-level measures are non-binding and intended to complement Member State policies through mutual learning, Erasmus+ actions, and funding support such as the European Social Fund Plus.

  • Adoption of an Education Package in Q4 2026 based on Articles 165, 166, 292, and 153(1)(b) and (i) TFEU.
  • Proposes a Communication and two non-binding Council Recommendations on basic skills and attractive teaching careers.
  • Establishes a European Digital Infrastructure Consortium (EDIC) for education to foster digital sovereignty.
  • Pillar 1 focuses on basic skills acceleration, tutoring, evidence-based teaching, and support for disadvantaged learners.
  • Pillar 2 addresses teacher shortages, working conditions, career development, recruitment, and retention.
  • Pillar 3 promotes sovereign digital infrastructure, AI literacy via the EU-OECD framework, online safety, and media literacy.

Affected, as named in the text: Member States; Member State authorities; Teachers and trainers; Learners; Disadvantaged groups (migrants, those facing ethnic or racial discrimination, LGBTIQ+ people, Roma, learners in disadvantaged areas, persons with disabilities); Schools and educational organisations; European EdTech sector.

What is asked: Stakeholders and the public are invited to comment on the Commission's understanding of the problems and possible solutions. Specifically, the Commission asks: which forms of EU-level support would best complement national, regional, and local actions to reverse basic skills decline and raise teaching attractiveness; how cooperation between schools and authorities can be strengthened to apply evidence-based methods; which digital tools, infrastructure, and practices are currently used and where the greatest needs, potential, and challenges lie for European-scale, digitally sovereign solutions; and what evidence, data sources, or best practices should inform the monitoring and evaluation frameworks.

Dossier summary from the portal

<p>Teacher shortages weaken Member States’ capacities to ensure high-quality, inclusive education. Driven by an ageing workforce, low career attractiveness and mounting workload, the teaching profession faces recruitment and retention challenges. Societal demands and the use of artificial intelligence require new pedagogical skills. This proposal for a Council Recommendation aims to complement Member States’ efforts to make teaching attractive, improve career paths and modernise professional training. </p><p>This initiative is part of the Education Package; the Call for evidence is published under “Quality Education for All: A 2040 Roadmap”.</p>
