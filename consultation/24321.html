Sustainable fishing in the EU: state of play and orientations for 2027

Full official titleCOMMUNICATION FROM THE COMMISSION TO THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND THE COUNCIL Sustainable fishing in the EU: state of play and orientations for 2027

closed Open for answers from 5 Jun 2026 to 31 Aug 2026. Answer on the Commission's site. Topics: Maritime affairs and fisheries.

What the Commission proposes

This Communication reports on progress towards achieving maximum sustainable yield (MSY) and sets out orientations to shape Commission proposals on fishing opportunities for 2027. It assesses the conservation status of fish stocks, the balance between fleet capacity and fishing opportunities, the socio-economic performance of the EU fleet, and the implementation of the landing obligation. The Commission calls on Member States to submit action plans for fleet segments facing overcapacity, to deploy selective gear, and to implement their national European Maritime, Fisheries and Aquaculture Fund (EMFAF) programmes. It also stresses the need to address wider environmental pressures and build on scientific advice to support long-term stock recovery and fleet profitability.

  • Fishing mortality is generally declining and compliance with MSY targets is progressing, but biomass indicators in several sea basins have not improved as expected.
  • Member States must submit action plans with adjustment targets and tools for fleet segments where fishing capacity exceeds fishing opportunities.
  • Member States are invited to deploy market-ready selective gears, limit fishing pressure on sensitive habitats and marine protected areas, and implement national EMFAF programmes.
  • Volatile fuel prices, delayed fleet decarbonisation, and reduced landings continue to weigh on incomes and undermine long-term profitability.
  • Efforts must continue to improve the socio-economic situation of fishers, modernise fleets, render working conditions attractive to new generations, and make the landing obligation more effective.
  • Actions under multiannual management plans such as the Western Mediterranean plan and GFCM strategy must continue to bring shared stocks to sustainable levels.

Affected, as named in the text: European Parliament; Council; Member States; coastal Member States; fishers; small scale fishers; EU fishing fleet; advisory councils; coastal communities.
