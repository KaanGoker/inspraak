Competitiveness in the single banking market

Full official titleCommunication from the Commission to the European Parliament, the European Council, the Council, the European Central Bank, the European Economic and Social Committee and the Committee of the Regions on the competitiveness of the Single Banking Market

closed Open for answers from 11 Feb 2026 to 11 Mar 2026. Answer on the Commission's site. Topics: Banking and financial services.

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission has published a Call for Evidence to prepare a Communication on competitiveness in the Single Banking Market scheduled for the third quarter of 2026. The initiative assesses how the EU banking sector supports economic competitiveness and examines structural barriers, regulatory complexity, and persistent fragmentation that hinder cross-border activity and integration. It evaluates whether current prudential, supervisory, and crisis-management frameworks remain fit for purpose amid digitalisation and global competitive pressures. The resulting Communication will identify targeted actions and inform potential future Commission initiatives and legislative proposals.

  • Follows up on the Savings and Investments Union Communication adopted on 19 March 2025.
  • Aims to prepare a Commission Communication scheduled for publication in Q3 2026.
  • Examines barriers from regulatory complexity, national discretions, lack of a common deposit protection system, and divergent supervisory practices.
  • Evaluates the fitness of prudential, supervisory, and crisis-management frameworks while maintaining financial stability and resilience.
  • Runs alongside a targeted consultation, reality-check meetings, high-level industry dialogues, and exchanges with EU institutions and agencies.
  • Provides a 4-week feedback period open to all interested stakeholders in the 24 official EU languages.

Affected, as named in the text: banks; investors; consumers; firms; strategically relevant European projects seeking financing; financial service providers; industry associations; supervisors; resolution authorities; promotional banks; civil society organisations; consumer organisations; public authorities; academic organisations; social partners; think tanks; interested citizens; scientific researchers; scientific associations with expertise in the banking sector.

What is asked: Stakeholders are invited to provide practical feedback and information on the competitiveness of the Single Market in banking, potential barriers affecting its functioning, and possible ways to improve integration, efficiency, and the EU regulatory and supervisory framework. Scientific researchers and academic organisations are also invited to submit relevant published and pre-print scientific research, analyses, and data synthesising the current state of knowledge.

Dossier summary from the portal

Announced in the Savings and Investments Union, the initiative aims to assess how the EU banking sector supports competitiveness of the EU economy and to identify targeted actions to address competitiveness issues, including structural barriers that limit cross-border activity, and financial market integration. It will evaluate whether the current prudential, supervisory and crisis-management frameworks remain fit for purpose in light of evolving market dynamics, digitalisation and global competitive pressures.
