EU emissions trading system for maritime, aviation and stationary installations, and market stability reserve - review

Full official titleProposal for a REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL amending Regulations (EU) 2015/757 and (EU) 2023/1805 to simplify and streamline monitoring, reporting and verification and to align with revisions of the EU Emissions Trading System

open on 5 Oct 2026 Open for answers from 20 Aug 2026 to 3 Nov 2026 (open). Answer on the Commission's site. Topics: Climate action.

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission proposes amending Regulations (EU) 2015/757 and (EU) 2023/1805 to simplify and streamline monitoring, reporting, and verification obligations for the maritime sector. The initiative introduces a single monitoring plan and a single report per ship covering both greenhouse gas emissions and energy use on board. It also aligns rules on accreditation, verification, and enforcement under Regulation (EU) 2015/757 to ensure coherent implementation across the EU Emissions Trading System and FuelEU Maritime. Additionally, the proposal adjusts calculation methodologies, formulas, and emission factors to reflect technological and scientific developments.

  • Requires shipping companies to submit a single monitoring plan and a single report per ship for greenhouse gas emissions and energy use.
  • Brings rules on accreditation, verification, and Member State enforcement under Regulation (EU) 2015/757.
  • Updates monitoring formulas to account for Well-to-Tank and Tank-to-Wake emissions, slip coefficients, and substitute energy sources.
  • Extends the scope to include specific categories of ships between 400 and 5,000 gross tonnage and those performing or supporting offshore operations.
  • Grants companies the right to use certified actual values instead of default tank-to-wake emission factors, except for fossil fuel CO2 factors.
  • Empowers the Commission to adopt delegated and implementing acts regarding automated systems, standard templates, and updates to monitoring annexes.

Affected, as named in the text: Shipping companies; Verifiers; Member States.

Dossier summary from the portal

The EU emissions trading system is a key policy tool to achieve the EU’s climate targets. Certain elements of the system are subject to review by 2026, including on assessing additional policies to reach those targets. In addition, the market stability reserve is also subject to review by 2026. Both are being evaluated to feed into these reviews.
