Quality Jobs Act

Full official titleLegislative initiative to improve working conditions, health and safety at work and implementation of workers’ rights

closed Open for answers from 1 Sep 2026 to 29 Sep 2026. Answer on the Commission's site. Topics: Employment and social affairs.

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission is preparing a legislative and non-legislative initiative called the Quality Jobs Act, slated for adoption in the fourth quarter of 2026. This initiative addresses modern challenges in the labor market resulting from digitalisation, decarbonisation, and new forms of work. It aims to improve working conditions, occupational safety and health, and the enforcement of workers' rights across the European Union. Furthermore, the act seeks to balance worker protection with business competitiveness and simplification.

  • Addresses the right to disconnect and telework to counter the 'always-on' work culture.
  • Examines algorithmic management and artificial intelligence at work to prevent excessive monitoring and protect well-being.
  • Reviews occupational safety and health directives regarding workplace and display screen equipment to address psychosocial, ergonomic, and climate-related risks.
  • Investigates the protection of workers' rights in complex subcontracting chains and with labour intermediaries.
  • Considers measures for just transitions, effective implementation and enforcement, and strengthening the role of social partners.
  • Aims to achieve regulatory simplification and reduce administrative burdens for businesses, particularly SMEs.

Affected, as named in the text: Workers; Employers; Small and micro-sized companies; SMEs.

What is asked: Respondents are asked to provide feedback on the Commission's understanding of the problems and possible solutions, and to share relevant information and data regarding the potential impacts of the different policy options.

Dossier summary from the portal

The initiative, announced in the 2025 State of the Union and the 2026 Commission Work Programme, aims at addressing new challenges and realities for workers in the modern economy. Working conditions and job quality are influenced by structural shifts in the labour market and increasing digitalisation and evolving forms of work and workplaces. Good and fair working conditions adapted to the modern economy are essential for sustainable competitiveness and quality jobs and should be ensured for any type of contract and across sectors.
