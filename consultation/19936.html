Green-listing certain waste for the purposes of shipments to recovery between Member States

Harmonised classification of waste to accelerate the transition to a Circular Economy

closed opc launched, Ares(2025)4387521, ENV. Feedback period 2 Jul 2025 to 31 Oct 2025. Initiative on the portal. Topics: Environment.

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission launched a public consultation to gather stakeholder information to prepare measures, via delegated acts under Regulation (EU) 2024/1157, to facilitate the shipment of specific waste streams subject to the green-list procedure for recovery between Member States. The initiative complements the Waste Shipment Regulation and future Circular Economy Act objectives by considering certain waste streams as green-listed and assessing possible contamination thresholds. Stakeholder feedback focused on whether to extend green-listing to specific non-hazardous electrical and electronic equipment waste, textile waste, and clean metal waste. The factual summary report compiles stakeholder contributions on eligible waste types, depollution criteria, and contamination thresholds without reflecting the official position of the Commission.

  • A total of 81% of respondents strongly agreed with extending the scope of green-listed waste for shipments between EU Member States.
  • A majority of respondents (56%) strongly agreed that more types of non-hazardous e-waste should be green-listed.
  • On textile waste, 44% of respondents expressed strong agreement with considering more non-hazardous textile waste as green-listed, while 40% did not know how to reply.
  • Stakeholder views on contamination thresholds for metal waste showed 37% did not know how to answer, 32% strongly agreed, and 10% strongly disagreed.
  • Stakeholders proposed green-listing specific depolluted e-waste components, including electric motors, metal scrap, photovoltaic panels, plastic-rich fractions, and cables.
  • Recurring criteria suggested by stakeholders include quantitative contamination thresholds, mandatory removal of batteries and capacitors, prior depollution, and proof of non-hazardous status.

Affected, as named in the text: companies and business associations; EU citizens; public authorities; NGOs; academic and research institutions; trade unions; consumer organizations.

What is asked: Stakeholders were invited to provide feedback on specific waste streams to be addressed, whether specific waste types currently subject to prior notification and consent should be green-listed for shipments within the EU, and criteria such as contamination thresholds to classify certain waste as green-listed rather than mixtures.

Dossier summary from the portal

The act implements the new Waste Shipment Regulation and complements the objectives of the future Circular Economy Act. It will consider certain waste as green-listed, and possibility introduce contamination thresholds for considering sone waste as green-listed. Adoption of the act will be preceded by a consultation processed to gather input from a wide range of stakeholders on waste streams to be addressed.
