Evaluation of the industrial and agricultural autonomous tariff suspensions and quotas scheme

Full official titleProposal for the evaluation of the Communication from the Commission concerning autonomous tariff suspensions and quotas (2011/C 363/02) and related base Council Regulations (EU) 2021/2283, 1388/2013, 2021/2278 and 1387/2013.

closed Open for answers from 10 Sep 2026 to 8 Oct 2026. Answer on the Commission's site. Topics: Customs.

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission has published a call for evidence to evaluate the autonomous tariff suspensions and quotas (ATSQ) scheme for industrial and agricultural goods. The evaluation will assess whether the scheme remains fit for purpose and continues to meet its core objectives of stimulating EU economic activity, boosting business competitiveness, and modernising production structures. It aims to prepare a revision to streamline administrative arrangements, clarify requirements for granting tariff relief, reduce the frequency of legal updates, and leverage digitalisation. The initiative covers the period since the last evaluation in 2013, with work scheduled to start in Q3-2026 and conclude in Q1-2027.

  • The evaluation covers autonomous tariff suspensions and quotas, which provide temporary tariff relief on imports of raw materials, semi-finished goods, or components unavailable in sufficient quantities in the EU.
  • In 2025, imports under the scheme represented approximately EUR 35.5 billion worth of goods and generated an estimated EUR 1.6 billion in duty savings for EU producers.
  • The evaluation will assess the scheme against five standard criteria: effectiveness, efficiency, relevance, coherence, and EU added value.
  • The findings will feed into a planned revision under the regulatory deep cleaning action plan to streamline administrative procedures and lower compliance costs.
  • Specific areas under review include requirements to grant tariff relief, the frequency of regular legal updates, and the application and assessment process.
  • The initiative is led by DG TAXUD (Unit B.5) and is scheduled to run from Q3-2026 to Q1-2027, following an external study conducted between October 2024 and April 2026.

Affected, as named in the text: EU businesses; EU producers; SMEs; businesses using the scheme; businesses involved all along the supply chain; business associations; customs authorities in the 27 EU Member States; Enterprise Europe Network members.

What is asked: The Commission invites feedback on the main challenges companies face under ATSQs, whether targeted duty relief on production inputs increases EU competitiveness, the costs for businesses to apply for, object to, and monitor measures, specific simplification measures to reduce administrative burdens while preserving policy objectives, potential incentives for EU producers to diversify sourcing of inputs, and any other relevant topics.
