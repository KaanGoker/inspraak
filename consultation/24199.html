Evaluation and Revision of the Chips Act ("Chips Act 2.0")

Full official titleProposal for a REGULATION OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL on a framework of measures for strengthening the Union’s semiconductor ecosystem, repealing Regulation (EU) 2023/1781 (Chips Act 2.0)

closed Open for answers from 2 Jul 2026 to 10 Sep 2026. Answer on the Commission's site. Topics: Digital economy and society.

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission proposes a regulation establishing the Chips Act 2.0 to repeal and replace Regulation (EU) 2023/1781. The initiative aims to strengthen the Union's semiconductor ecosystem and economic security by addressing vulnerabilities across the value chain, particularly in advanced manufacturing and artificial intelligence chips. It provides technical and financial frameworks under the Chips for Europe Initiative 2.0 to expand design capacities via a cloud-based design platform and support pilot lines bridging the lab-to-fab transition. The proposal also improves coordination mechanisms between Member States and the Commission while supporting strategic projects and first-of-a-kind facilities.

  • Repeals Regulation (EU) 2023/1781 to establish an updated regulatory framework for the Union's semiconductor ecosystem.
  • Establishes a cloud-based design platform providing design tools, libraries, IP, and pilot line Process Design Kits to support fabless start-ups, SMEs, and researchers.
  • Supports five existing pilot lines (sub-2 nm logic, FD-SOI, advanced packaging and heterogeneous integration, wide-bandgap materials, and photonic integrated circuits) while allowing upgrades and new testbeds.
  • Introduces Lab to Fab Accelerators to speed up industrial uptake of pilot line technologies, particularly in advanced packaging.
  • Finances the Chips for Europe Initiative 2.0 via Horizon Europe, the Digital Europe Programme, and national and private funding.
  • Improves Pillar III coordination mechanisms between Member States and the Commission to foster security of supply and ecosystem resilience.

Affected, as named in the text: start-ups; SMEs; scaleups; fabless start-ups; Member States; research, education, and industry actors; European universities; cloud providers; Electronic Design Automation (EDA) vendors; IP providers; foundries and industrial manufacturing facilities; lead and user industries (health, mobility, energy, telecommunications, security, defence and space).

Dossier summary from the portal

The EU's competitiveness is tied to semiconductors. The Chips Act was successful in attracting a number of manufacturing investments and in launching activities aiming at bridging the gap from the laboratory to the factory. Yet challenges remain, especially in advanced manufacturing and Artificial Intelligence chips. Further steps are necessary to strengthen the EU's role in a wide selection of chips technologies and the full semiconductor value chain including materials, equipment, design and manufacturing.
