Biotech Act

Full official titlePublic Questionnaire informing the European Biotech Act

closed Open for answers from 4 Aug 2025 to 10 Nov 2025. Answer on the Commission's site. Topics: Food safety, Public health.

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission's Biotech Act initiative aims to propose measures that create an enabling environment to accelerate the transition of biotechnology products and applications from laboratory to factory and market, while maintaining high safety standards. This document provides a factual summary of 464 stakeholder contributions gathered through a public consultation informing the planned initiative. Feedback outlines stakeholder assessments of the EU regulatory framework, identifying significant barriers in testing, scaling-up, and market authorisation compared to third countries. In addition, respondents shared insights on operational challenges in biomanufacturing, as well as emerging risks and opportunities in biodefence and biosecurity.

  • A total of 464 contributions and 153 attachments were received from EU and non-EU stakeholders between August and November 2025.
  • A large majority of respondents agree that biotechnology positively impacts the EU economy (91%), society (90%), and the environment (79%).
  • Three-quarters of respondents (77%) report regulatory barriers in obtaining market authorisation, with majorities also reporting obstacles in testing, commercialisation, and scale-up.
  • Only 9-10% believe the EU regulatory environment enables faster market access, lower compliance costs, or less complexity compared to non-EU countries.
  • Top biomanufacturing challenges identified include global competition, lengthy permitting processes, pilot-to-industrial scale-up difficulties, and high energy and raw material costs.
  • Key biodefence challenges include risks to strategic autonomy in biomanufacturing (50%) and cybersecurity (45%), alongside opportunities in innovative medical countermeasures (45%).

Affected, as named in the text: companies/businesses; SMEs (micro-, small-, and medium-sized enterprises); large enterprises; business associations; non-governmental organisations (NGOs); academic/research institutions; EU citizens; non-EU citizens; public authorities (national, regional, local, and international); trade unions; consumer associations; environmental organisations; private investors; clusters and cluster organisations.

What is asked: Stakeholders were asked to share views across nine areas concerning biotechnology and the Biotech Act, specifically evaluating general impacts, regulatory barriers across development and commercialisation phases, competitiveness relative to non-EU frameworks, access to capital, clusters, manufacturing challenges, skills, data and artificial intelligence, and defence and security applications.

Dossier summary from the portal

The biotech sector is research-driven, fast-moving and requires substantial and continuous public and private investments. Biotechnology is an enabling technology, which plays a critical role in several areas of the EU economy. The Biotech Act will propose a series of measures aiming at creating an enabling environment to accelerate the transition of biotech products and applications from the laboratory to factory and to the market while maintaining the highest safety standards for the protection of the population and the environment.
