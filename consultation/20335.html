Evaluation and Revision of the Chips Act ("Chips Act 2.0")

Regulation (EU) 202X/XXXX of the European Parliament and of the Council of XXXX establishing a framework of measures for strengthening Europe’s semiconductor ecosystem and amending Regulation (EU) 2023/1781 (Chips Act 2) (Text with EEA relevance)

closed cfe impact assess, Ares(2025)7291209, CNECT. Feedback period 5 Sep 2025 to 28 Nov 2025. Initiative on the portal. Topics: Digital economy and society.

What the Commission proposes

The European Commission presents a call for evidence for an evaluation and impact assessment run in parallel to review and amend Regulation (EU) 2023/1781 (Chips Act). The initiative aims to reduce EU dependency on third countries for leading-edge semiconductor nodes below 10 nanometres and increase insight into supply chain resilience and technology leakage risks. It explores targeted amendments to define strategic projects, set selection criteria, and potentially tap into future funding schemes beyond existing State aid rules. The evaluation will also assess opportunities to simplify procedures and reduce regulatory burdens for first-of-a-kind facilities.

  • Evaluates the existing Chips Act ahead of the mandatory 2026 review to address shortcomings and assess Better Regulation criteria.
  • Tackles the lack of EU manufacturing capacity for advanced nodes below 10 nanometres needed for strategic sectors such as defence, automotive, space, and high-performance computing.
  • Aims to protect Europe's competitive edge in mainstream semiconductors like power electronics, microcontrollers, photonics, and sensors against non-market practices.
  • Proposes to improve understanding of supply chain resilience, economic security, and technology leakage risks among key market actors.
  • Considers targeted amendments to define strategic projects, establish selection criteria, and enable access to future funding schemes.
  • Explores simplification and burden reduction in the State aid application process for first-of-a-kind facilities.

Affected, as named in the text: Member States; Key market actors; Strategic sectors (defence, security, automotive, space, high-performance computing).

What is asked: Respondents are invited to provide evidence and feedback to feed into the evaluation of the Chips Act's implementation and the parallel impact assessment on policy options to strengthen the EU semiconductor ecosystem, address dependencies in leading-edge chips, and reduce regulatory burdens.

Dossier summary from the portal

The EU's competitiveness is tied to semiconductors. The Chips Act was successful in attracting a number of manufacturing investments and in launching activities aiming at bridging the gap from the laboratory to the factory. Yet challenges remain, especially in advanced manufacturing and Artificial Intelligence chips. Further steps are necessary to strengthen the EU's role in a wide selection of chips technologies and the full semiconductor value chain including materials, equipment, design and manufacturing.
