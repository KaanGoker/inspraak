Competitiveness in the single banking market

Full official titleCOMMUNICATION FROM THE COMMISSION TO THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT, THE COUNCIL, THE EUROPEAN CENTRAL BANK, THE EUROPEAN ECONOMIC AND SOCIAL COMMITEE AND THE COMMITTEE OF THE REGIONS Competitiveness of the Banking Sector and the Single Market in Banking

closed Open for answers from 17 Jul 2026 to 17 Sep 2026. Answer on the Commission's site. Topics: Banking and financial services.

What the Commission proposes

The Commission sets out a forward-looking strategy under the Savings and Investments Union to enhance the competitiveness and integration of the EU banking sector while preserving financial stability. It proposes rebalancing the regulatory framework away from a highly risk-averse approach toward measured risk-taking, reducing gold-plating and national discretion to build a genuine single rulebook. The initiative also aims to deepen market integration through cross-border activity and consolidation, accompanied by safeguards to protect financial stability across all Member States. In addition, the Commission will reinforce regular reviews of the banking system and hold structured discussions with supervisory and resolution authorities to assess their impact on the internal market.

  • Rebalance the regulatory and supervisory framework from a zero-risk-tolerance culture to one supporting responsible and measured risk-taking.
  • Strengthen the single rulebook by eliminating gold-plating, divergent national implementation, reporting duplication, and fragmented national rules.
  • Foster cross-border market integration, structural consolidation, and shared infrastructures with safeguards balancing home and host Member State interests.
  • Carefully calibrate bank capital requirements when simplifying rules, harmonising methodologies, removing overlaps, and applying proportionality.
  • Reinforce the Commission's monitoring of banking competitiveness and its impact on the internal market.
  • Organise regular discussions with the Single Supervisory Mechanism and the Single Resolution Board on the functioning of the banking system.

Affected, as named in the text: EU households; large corporates; small and medium-sized enterprises; banks; savers and investors; non-bank financial institutions; Single Supervisory Mechanism (SSM); Single Resolution Mechanism (SRM) / Single Resolution Board (SRB); European Banking Authority (EBA); European Systemic Risk Board (ESRB); Member States.

Dossier summary from the portal

Announced in the Savings and Investments Union, the initiative aims to assess how the EU banking sector supports competitiveness of the EU economy and to identify targeted actions to address competitiveness issues, including structural barriers that limit cross-border activity, and financial market integration. It will evaluate whether the current prudential, supervisory and crisis-management frameworks remain fit for purpose in light of evolving market dynamics, digitalisation and global competitive pressures.
